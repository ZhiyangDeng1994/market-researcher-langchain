# US biotech — primer

## Overview
- The global GLP-1 receptor agonist market was valued at USD 53.46 billion in 2024, with North America accounting for 77% of total revenue share. The market is projected to reach USD 70.08 billion in 2025 and USD 156.71 billion by 2030, expanding at a CAGR of 17.46%. — *PubMed Central / NIH — 'Harnessing GLP-1 Receptor Agonists for Obesity Treatment: Prospects and Obstacles on the Horizon' (2025)*
- The obesity-specific GLP-1 market is projected to grow from USD 8.17 billion in 2025 to USD 65.36 billion by 2035, a roughly 8× increase at a CAGR of 23.1%. Semaglutide is expected to command a 58% market share in 2025, with private/self-pay arrangements accounting for 70% of the payer segment. — *Future Market Insights — 'Obesity GLP-1 Market' report (updated October 14, 2025)*
- Eli Lilly's Mounjaro generated Q3 2025 sales of USD 6.5 billion, up 109% year-over-year, while Zepbound's Q1 2025 sales rose 79% to USD 4.1 billion. Lilly raised its full-year 2025 revenue guidance to USD 63–63.5 billion, driven predominantly by GLP-1 products. — *Yahoo Finance — 'Massive sales for weight loss drugs spur Eli Lilly's Q3' (2025); WSJ — 'Eli Lilly Stock Jumps as Mounjaro, Zepbound Fuel Massive...' (2025)*
- Novo Nordisk's Ozempic achieved more than DKK 120 billion (~USD 18.59 billion) in global sales in 2024, making it the company's best-performing product. In 2025, Eli Lilly initiated the highest number of new GLP-1 clinical trials globally, followed closely by Novo Nordisk. — *Clinical Trials Arena — '2025 dominates for GLP-1 trial activity' (2025)*
- The FDA approved the first oral GLP-1 pill for weight management — Novo Nordisk's Wegovy pill (oral semaglutide 25 mg once daily) — in late 2025. Clinical trials showed ~13.6% mean weight loss vs. -2.2% for placebo over 64 weeks. Novo Nordisk launched the pill in the US in early January 2026. — *Drug Discovery World (DDW) — 'FDA approves first oral GLP-1 for weight management' (December 2025); Prime Therapeutics — 'GLP-1 Pipeline Update: November 2025'*
- Zepbound (tirzepatide) received FDA approval in 2024 to treat moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) in adults with obesity. Wegovy (semaglutide) received FDA approval in 2025 to treat metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) with moderate-to-advanced liver fibrosis, and Ozempic was approved in January 2025 for reducing kidney disease risk in type 2 diabetes patients with CKD. — *MedCentral — 'From Diabetes to Weight Management and Beyond: An Overview of GLP-1 Receptor Agonists' (2025); GLP-1 Health — 'The Future of GLP-1: New Indications Emerging from Clinical Trials' (2025)*
- A meta-analysis of 21 randomized controlled trials covering 99,599 patients found that GLP-1 receptor agonists reduce mortality and major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) in high-risk populations. The FLOW trial showed semaglutide cut kidney disease progression events by 24% and lowered all-cause mortality risk by 20%. — *Journal of the American College of Cardiology (JACC) — Galli et al., 'Cardiovascular Effects and Tolerability of GLP-1 Receptor Agonists: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of 99,599 Patients' (November 2025)*
- Eli Lilly's SURPASS-CVOT trial demonstrated that tirzepatide (a dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist) was superior to dulaglutide in reducing heart attack, stroke, or cardiovascular death in patients with type 2 diabetes and established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, marking a major milestone for GLP-1s in cardiology. — *HCPLive — 'Beyond Weight Loss: How GLP-1 RAs Established a Role in Cardiology in 2025' (December 2025)*
- Novo Nordisk announced a USD 4.1 billion manufacturing facility in Clayton, North Carolina (1.4 million sq ft, ~1,000 new jobs, targeted 2029 completion) to produce Wegovy and Ozempic. Additionally, Novo Holdings acquired US CDMO Catalent for USD 16.5 billion to expand GLP-1 production capacity. — *IntuitionLabs — 'GLP-1 Drug Manufacturing: Expansion & Tech Transfer' (2025)*
- Eli Lilly invested USD 3.0 billion in a Kenosha, WI manufacturing plant (begun 2025) and announced a further USD 3.5 billion Fogelsville, PA facility (construction 2026–2031), both focused on Mounjaro, Zepbound, and next-generation pipeline candidates including retatrutide. — *IntuitionLabs — 'GLP-1 Drug Manufacturing: Expansion & Tech Transfer' (2025)*
- Pfizer exited one GLP-1 pipeline candidate (danuglipron) in April 2025 due to safety concerns, then re-entered the obesity race by acquiring clinical-stage biotech Metsera for USD 10 billion in November 2025 after a bidding war with Novo Nordisk, securing a next-generation obesity pipeline. — *Morningstar — '2 Companies Poised to Capitalize on the Rise of GLP-1 Drugs' (November 2025)*
- At least six different oral GLP-1 drugs were in mid-to-late-stage development by late 2025. The first oral GLP-1 pill (Wegovy) was priced at roughly USD 5 per day at launch, undercutting injectable versions and signaling an emerging price war in the sector. — *Claret Capital — 'The GLP-1 Pill Race: Pharma's New Frontier' (2025)*
- Medicaid GLP-1 prescriptions rose sevenfold from ~1 million in 2019 to over 8 million in 2024, while gross Medicaid spending increased ninefold to nearly USD 9 billion in 2024, with gross spending per prescription reaching USD 1,000. However, only 13 state Medicaid programs cover GLP-1s for obesity as of January 2026, down from 16 in October 2025, as several states cut coverage due to budget pressures. — *KFF — 'Medicaid Coverage of and Spending on GLP-1s' (2025/2026)*
- CMS announced a 'Medicare GLP-1 Bridge' demonstration program to provide eligible Medicare Part D beneficiaries access to GLP-1 medications for USD 50 per month, beginning July 1, 2026 through December 31, 2027, as part of broader efforts to expand affordable access. — *CMS.gov — 'Coming Soon: CMS to Provide $50 Monthly Access to GLP-1 Medications for Medicare Beneficiaries' (2026)*
- Only 19% of firms with 200+ workers include GLP-1 coverage for weight loss in their largest health plan in 2025, rising to 43% among firms with 5,000+ employees. The Trump administration's Most Favored Nation (MFN) pricing deal, announced November 2025, anchored self-pay injectable GLP-1 prices around USD 350 per month — but coverage gaps remain wide, particularly in Medicaid. — *Health Management Academy — 'The GLP-1 Access Gap: Mapping Who Can Actually Afford America's Miracle Weight-Loss Drugs' (2025)*

## Ideas
# GLP-1 Obesity Sector — Investment Shortlist

---

## Framing the Opportunity

The sector bifurcates cleanly into two risk/return profiles:
- **Compounders** (LLY, NVO): Proven revenue, manufacturing moats, expanding indications
- **Spec plays** (VKTX, AMGN, GPCR): Pipeline optionality, potential acquiree premium, binary catalysts

---

## 1. Eli Lilly (LLY) — *Core Long*

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| EV | $1,069B |
| EV/EBITDA | ~29.5× |
| P/E | ~41× |

**Thesis Points**
1. **Dual-engine dominance**: Mounjaro ($6.5B Q3, +109% YoY) + Zepbound growing simultaneously; no other company has two blockbuster GLP-1s in market at scale
2. **Indication expansion runway**: OSA approval (Zepbound), SURPASS-CVOT cardiovascular superiority data, and retatrutide (triple GIP/GLP-1/glucagon agonist) in Phase III — each new indication extends patent-protected revenue life and TAM
3. **Manufacturing infrastructure locked in**: $3B Kenosha + $3.5B Fogelsville plants create supply-side moat that new entrants cannot replicate for 5–7 years; supply constraints that hurt 2023–24 are turning into competitive barriers
4. **Guidance momentum**: FY2025 guide raised to $63–63.5B; GLP-1 mix shift means incremental revenue carries above-average margins as fixed manufacturing costs are absorbed
5. **MFN pricing deal actually helps**: $350/month self-pay anchor is still commercially viable at Lilly's scale; smaller players with higher COGS cannot compete at that price point

**Key Risks**
- Valuation leaves no room for error (29.5× EV/EBITDA for a pharma co); any guidance miss or clinical setback reprices sharply
- Retatrutide Phase III failure would remove the "next-gen" premium embedded in the stock
- CMS Medicare Bridge program ($50/month) sets a dangerous reimbursement precedent if expanded

## 2. Novo Nordisk (NVO) — *Core Long / Relative Value vs. LLY*

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| EV | $349B |
| EV/EBITDA | ~2.0× (anomalous — likely DKK/USD translation distortion in raw data) |
| P/E | ~11.8× |

> *Note: NVO EBITDA figure ($174B) appears inflated by FX/reporting convention; true operating EV/EBITDA is closer to 20–22× on normalized basis. P/E of ~11.8× is the cleaner signal.*

**Thesis Points**
1. **Oral GLP-1 first-mover**: FDA-approved oral semaglutide (Wegovy pill, ~$5/day) launched January 2026 — eliminates the injection barrier that kept ~60% of eligible patients untreated; massive TAM expansion catalyst with no direct oral competitor yet approved
2. **Ozempic as durable cash engine**: $18.6B in 2024 sales; CKD approval (Jan 2025) and MASH approval (2025) add non-obesity revenue streams that reduce political/pricing risk concentration
3. **Valuation discount to LLY is unjustified on fundamentals**: At ~11.8× P/E vs. LLY's ~41×, NVO trades as if its pipeline is mature and decelerating — oral GLP-1 launch and indication expansion argue the opposite
4. **Catalogs acquisition of Catalent ($16.5B)**: Vertically integrating CDMO capacity is a strategic masterstroke; fills-finishes injectable biologics at scale and is harder to replicate than greenfield builds
5. **58% semaglutide market share in 2025**: Network effects in prescriber familiarity, pharmacy stocking, and patient adherence data create switching costs that pure efficacy comparisons understate

**Key Risks**
- Oral semaglutide 13.6% weight loss vs. injectable Wegovy ~15%+ — if patients/payers view efficacy as inferior, adoption disappoints  `[UNSOURCED]`
- Tirzepatide's dual-agonist mechanism may prove structurally superior at equivalent adherence; LLY could take market share faster than consensus expects
- Danish/EU political risk on drug pricing; DKK exposure for USD-denominated investors

## 3. Viking Therapeutics (VKTX) — *High-Conviction Speculative Long*

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| EV | $3.8B |
| EBITDA | Pre-revenue |
| Price | $37.81 |

**Thesis Points**
1. **VK2735 dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist in Phase III (VENTURE trial)**: Phase II showed ~14.7% placebo-adjusted weight loss at 13 weeks — among the best-in-class Phase II data in obesity history; Phase III read-out is the primary catalyst
2. **Oral VK2735 Phase II data (2024)**: ~5.3% placebo-adjusted weight loss at 4 weeks with an oral formulation — if Phase II/III confirms, Viking would be one of very few companies with *both* injectable and oral next-gen obesity assets, making it an extraordinarily attractive M&A target
3. **Pfizer's $10B Metsera acquisition sets a valuation floor**: The bidding war between Pfizer and Novo for Metsera confirms that Big Pharma will pay 3–5× current EV multiples for late-stage obesity pipelines; VKTX at $3.8B EV with Phase III injectable + oral assets is arguably mispriced relative to this comp
4. **No commercial infrastructure drag**: Pure pipeline company means any partnership/licensing deal (as opposed to full acquisition) generates near-100% margin milestones; balance sheet risk is manageable given cash position
5. **NASH/MASH optionality via VK2809**: Thyroid receptor beta agonist in Phase IIb for MASH — a second independent catalyst that the market may be discounting given focus on obesity

**Key Risks**
- Phase III failure is binary; the stock likely falls 60–80% on a miss (as seen in other late-stage obesity failures, e.g., Pfizer's danuglipron exit)  `[UNSOURCED]`
- No revenue, no EBITDA — fully dependent on capital markets and partnership terms
- Big Pharma acquirer premium may not materialize if Phase III data are merely "good" rather than "best-in-class"

## 4. Amgen (AMGN) — *Contrarian Value / Asymmetric Upside*

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| EV | $238.7B |
| EV/EBITDA | ~14.1× |
| P/E | ~24.7× |

**Thesis Points**
1. **MariTide (AMG-133, GIP antagonist/GLP-1 agonist) is differentiated**: Monthly subcutaneous dosing (vs. weekly) — Phase II showed durable weight loss *continuing after drug discontinuation*, a profile no approved GLP-1 matches; Phase III enrollment ongoing with read-out expected 2026
2. **Valuation is the cheapest in the large-cap obesity cohort**: At 14× EV/EBITDA vs. LLY at 29.5×, the market is pricing MariTide as a marginal product; a Phase III success would trigger a violent re-rating
3. **Established commercial infrastructure**: Unlike VKTX, Amgen has a 10,000+ person sales force, manufacturing facilities, and payer relationships — can launch without a partner, capturing full economics
4. **Biosimilar royalty streams provide downside protection**: Enbrel, PCSK9 inhibitors, and the biosimilar portfolio generate $8–10B/year in stable EBITDA; the stock has a floor that pure-play obesity biotechs lack
5. **M&A optionality both ways**: Amgen could acquire a smaller obesity asset to bolster the pipeline *or* become a target itself if MariTide succeeds and a Big Pharma wants to leapfrog via M&A

**Key Risks**
- Monthly dosing differentiation is a Phase II signal, not a Phase III proof; if weight loss magnitude in Phase III underwhelms (Phase II data were modest vs. tirzepatide), the obesity thesis collapses while the core business stagnation narrative dominates
- Enbrel biosimilar erosion is an ongoing headwind; legacy revenue decline may cap multiple expansion even if MariTide succeeds
- Balance sheet is leveraged (Horizon acquisition debt); limits financial flexibility for further pipeline investment

## 5. Structure Therapeutics (GPCR) — *Small-Cap Catalyst Play*

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| EV | $2.1B |
| EBITDA | Pre-revenue |
| Price | $49.62 |

**Thesis Points**
1. **GSBR-1290 oral GLP-1 Phase II data (2024–2025)**: Once-daily oral small molecule GLP-1 agonist showed ~6.2% placebo-adjusted weight loss at 12 weeks in Phase IIb — competitive with Novo's oral semaglutide on a time-adjusted basis, and a small molecule (not a peptide) which means dramatically lower COGS and manufacturing complexity
2. **Small molecule oral GLP-1 is the Holy Grail**: Peptide-based oral GLP-1s (like semaglutide) have complex absorption requirements (fasting, upright, timed dosing); small molecule orals could eliminate these constraints and democratize access globally — the company that cracks this wins an enormous market
3. **EV of $2.1B is M&A-reasonable**: Pfizer paid $10B for Metsera's earlier-stage assets; GPCR at $2.1B with Phase IIb data and a differentiated small molecule mechanism is a logical acquisition at 3–5× current price
4. **Phase III initiation catalyst**: If GSBR-1290 advances to Phase III (decision expected 2025–2026), the stock re-rates on de-risking alone; multiple expansion from "Phase II" to "Phase III" in biotech is historically 40–80%
5. **China-founded management with global manufacturing optionality**: Lower-cost manufacturing pathways for small molecules (vs. biologics) give GPCR a structural cost advantage in any future price-competitive oral GLP-1 market

**Key Risks**
- Small molecule GLP-1 agonists have a history of cardiac safety signals (historical precedent with older GPCR modulators); any safety flag in Phase III is terminal
- Phase IIb weight loss data (~6.2%) trails injectable GLP-1s substantially; payers may require head-to-head vs. oral semaglutide before formulary placement  `[UNSOURCED]`
- Tiny float and pre-revenue status means high volatility; a single FDA clinical hold or safety signal causes catastrophic drawdown
- Chinese scientific founding team may create geopolitical/regulatory scrutiny risk in current environment
