# US cloud computing — primer

## Overview
- The global cloud computing market was valued at approximately $912.77 billion in 2025 and is projected to grow to $1,106.28 billion in 2026, expanding at a CAGR of 20.61% through 2035 to reach roughly $5.95 trillion. — *Precedence Research, 2025*
- North America dominated global cloud computing in 2025, holding a 52% market share worth approximately $406 billion, with the US market alone projected to reach $282.62 billion in 2026. — *Fortune Business Insights, 2025*
- Amazon (AWS), Microsoft (Azure), and Alphabet (Google Cloud) collectively accounted for 66% of global cloud infrastructure spending in Q3 2025, maintaining their top-three rankings. AWS led with $37.6 billion in Q1 2026 quarterly revenue, Google Cloud reached $20 billion, and Microsoft's cloud segment hit $34.7 billion. — *IEEE ComSoc Technology Blog / Yahoo Finance (Financial Times compilation), 2025–2026*
- The four largest hyperscalers — Amazon, Microsoft, Alphabet, and Meta — spent a combined $381 billion in capital expenditures in 2025, up from prior-year levels, and are collectively on track to spend approximately $725 billion in 2026 — a roughly 77% year-over-year surge. — *Yahoo Finance (Financial Times compilation), 2026*
- Individual 2026 capex guidance from major hyperscalers: Amazon at $200 billion (more than doubling its 2025 outlay of $131.8 billion), Alphabet at $175–$185 billion (up from $91.4 billion in 2025), Microsoft at ~$145–$190 billion, and Meta at $115–$135 billion (up from $72.2 billion in 2025). — *Yahoo Finance / Forbes, 2026*
- Global data center capital expenditures surged 57% in 2025 as AI deployments accelerated, with hyperscalers and AI model developers expected to sustain strong capex momentum into 2026. — *Dell'Oro Group via Yahoo Finance, March 2026*
- Wells Fargo estimates total hyperscaler compute capacity reached 49 gigawatts at end-2025, with Alphabet, Amazon, and Microsoft accounting for ~80% (39 GW). Capacity is projected to double to 98 GW by 2027, with hyperscaler capex nearly doubling to over $860 billion by 2027 and total 2026–2028 spending reaching $2.47 trillion — about 8% above consensus. — *Wells Fargo via Yahoo Finance, 2026*
- Goldman Sachs projects US data center power demand to climb from 31 GW in 2025 to 41 GW in 2026 and 66 GW in 2027 — more than doubling in two years — with data centers' share of US peak summer power demand rising from 4.1% in 2025 to 8.5% in 2027. — *Goldman Sachs Research, 2026*
- The International Energy Agency found that data centers drove half of all growth in US electricity consumption in 2025. Data centers' share of total US electricity consumption is projected to climb from 3.7% in 2023 to nearly 12% by 2030, requiring over $500 billion in grid infrastructure investment. — *IEA / NaturalNews.com, April 2026*
- Global cloud infrastructure services spending reached $399.6 billion for full-year 2025 — a 24% year-over-year growth rate — with Q4 2025 alone rising 29% year-over-year to $110.9 billion, marking six consecutive quarters of 20%-or-higher growth. Omdia forecasts a further 27% increase in 2026, pushing total annual cloud spend above $500 billion. — *Omdia via IEEE ComSoc Technology Blog, 2025–2026*
- AWS grew revenue 28% year-over-year to $37.6 billion in Q1 2026, its fastest growth pace in 15 quarters, as enterprises accelerated adoption of generative AI workloads. Amazon's cloud backlog stood at $364 billion at end of Q1 2026, with 62% tied to commitments for its custom Trainium AI chips. — *UBS / Yahoo Finance, 2026*
- Google Cloud's backlog surged to a record $462 billion, while Google Cloud quarterly revenue hit $20 billion in Q1 2026, growing faster on a percentage basis than both AWS and Azure — reflecting strong enterprise demand for AI infrastructure. — *Yahoo Finance / Forbes, 2026*
- Bank of America forecasts global hyperscale spending rising 67% in 2025 and a further 31% in 2026, with total outlays climbing to $611 billion. It projects annual AI data center investment to triple to more than $1.2 trillion by 2030, with the AI infrastructure buildout approaching the same cash-flow strain seen at the peak of the 5G telecom buildout. — *Yahoo Finance (Bank of America analysis), 2025–2026*
- Sequoia's David Cahn identified an approximately $600 billion annual revenue gap between what hyperscalers are spending on AI infrastructure and what the broader AI ecosystem generates in actual sales — a gap that is widening in 2026 as capex acceleration outpaces revenue projections, drawing increasing scrutiny from financial markets. — *Forbes (Jason Kirsch), June 2026*
- Meta reported full-year 2025 capex of $72.2 billion, nearly double 2024's $39.2 billion, and guided for $115–$135 billion in 2026 driven by Meta SuperIntelligence Labs. Microsoft's Q4 2025 capex also exceeded Wall Street expectations, though its stock fell sharply on investor concerns about the surging spending burden. — *Yahoo Finance, January 2026*

## Ideas
# US Cloud Computing — Hyperscaler Capex Cycle: Investment Shortlist

---

## Framing the Opportunity

The $725B+ combined 2026 hyperscaler capex commitment creates a durable, multi-year spending wave. The best risk/reward sits in **picks-and-shovels suppliers** with pricing power and contracted backlogs rather than the hyperscalers themselves (whose capex *is* the cost). Key filters: (1) direct exposure to GPU/compute buildout, (2) power infrastructure scarcity, (3) manageable valuation vs. growth.

---

## 1. NVDA — NVIDIA Corporation
**EV/EBITDA: 30.8x | P/E: 32.4x**

### Thesis Points
- **Monopoly position in AI training and inference**: H100/H200/Blackwell GPUs are the default compute substrate for hyperscaler AI workloads; AWS's $364B backlog has 62% tied to *custom* chips, yet NVIDIA retains the majority of third-party GPU demand across all four hyperscalers
- **Software moat compounds hardware advantage**: CUDA ecosystem lock-in means switching costs are extraordinarily high; software revenue (DGX Cloud, NIM microservices) adds recurring, higher-margin streams atop hardware cycles
- **Data center revenue trajectory**: Hyperscaler capex doubling to $725B in 2026 flows disproportionately through GPU procurement; NVIDIA's data center segment has grown faster than overall hyperscaler capex spend
- **Sovereign AI and enterprise tailwinds provide diversification**: Beyond the four US hyperscalers, sovereign AI programs across 40+ countries and enterprise on-prem AI clusters create demand floors independent of any single customer
- **Blackwell architecture extends the upgrade cycle**: Successive architectural generations (Blackwell → Rubin) compress customer upgrade cycles, sustaining elevated ASPs and preventing demand air pockets

### Key Risks
- **Custom silicon displacement**: Amazon Trainium, Google TPUs, and Microsoft Maia could structurally reduce NVIDIA's hyperscaler wallet share over a 3–5 year horizon
- **Export control escalation**: BIS restrictions on H20/A800 class chips to China have already cost billions in revenue; further tightening is a binary policy risk
- **Valuation pricing perfection**: At 30x EV/EBITDA, any demand disappointment or inventory correction (as seen in 2022) produces outsized multiple compression
- **Concentration risk**: ~40%+ of revenue tied to a handful of hyperscaler customers whose own capex discipline is subject to macro or ROI scrutiny

---

## 2. AVGO — Broadcom Inc.
**EV/EBITDA: 45.6x | P/E: 65.0x**

### Thesis Points
- **Custom ASIC (XPU) franchise is structurally underappreciated**: Google's TPUs and Meta's MTIA are designed *with* Broadcom; as hyperscalers diversify away from NVIDIA, Broadcom captures that spend — making it a hedge and a beneficiary simultaneously
- **VMware integration creates durable software cashflow**: Post-acquisition VMware contributes high-margin recurring revenue that partially insulates Broadcom from semiconductor cyclicality, improving earnings quality
- **Networking is the non-obvious bottleneck**: As GPU clusters scale to 100,000+ chip pods, Ethernet/InfiniBand switching and custom SerDes become critical; Broadcom's networking silicon (Tomahawk, Jericho) is deeply embedded in hyperscaler spine architectures
- **Secular semiconductor content growth**: Each successive AI cluster generation requires more networking bandwidth per GPU, expanding Broadcom's dollar content per rack independent of unit volumes
- **Shareholder returns discipline**: Management has demonstrated consistent dividend growth and buybacks even through heavy M&A, providing downside support

### Key Risks
- **XPU customer concentration**: Google and Meta represent an outsized share of custom ASIC revenue; loss of either relationship would be materially negative
- **VMware integration execution**: $61B acquisition creates integration complexity and elevated leverage; enterprise customer pushback on pricing changes is an emerging headwind
- **Valuation demands flawless execution**: 45x EV/EBITDA leaves no room for margin misses or growth deceleration
- **Competitive threat from Marvell**: Marvell is aggressively pursuing custom ASIC mandates from Amazon and Microsoft, threatening Broadcom's quasi-monopoly in XPU design

---

## 3. VST — Vistra Corp.
**EV/EBITDA: 11.3x | P/E: 26.5x**

### Thesis Points
- **Power is the binding constraint on the entire buildout**: Goldman Sachs projects US data center power demand more than doubling from 31 GW (2025) to 66 GW (2027); Vistra owns *dispatchable* generation in the highest-demand ERCOT and PJM markets where data centers are clustering
- **Lowest valuation in the AI infrastructure complex**: At 11x EV/EBITDA vs. 30x+ for semiconductor names, Vistra offers the cheapest entry point for a company with direct, contracted exposure to hyperscaler power demand
- **Nuclear fleet provides 24/7 carbon-free power premium**: Microsoft and other hyperscalers are willing to pay above-market rates for clean, firm power for sustainability commitments; Vistra's nuclear assets (including the Comanche Peak acquisition) command premium PPAs
- **Retail electricity business provides earnings stability**: Vistra's retail segment smooths wholesale power price volatility, creating a more predictable earnings base than pure merchant generators
- **Structural undersupply of dispatchable capacity**: Retirements of coal/gas plants combined with data center demand growth create a multi-year supply/demand imbalance favorable to existing generators

### Key Risks
- **Regulatory and rate case risk**: FERC interconnection queues, state PUC decisions, and potential capacity market reforms could compress merchant power margins
- **Commodity price exposure**: Vistra's earnings remain partially correlated to natural gas prices; a sustained commodity downturn compresses margins despite structural demand growth
- **Hyperscaler capex deceleration risk**: If the $600B AI revenue gap identified by Sequoia forces hyperscalers to slow buildout, near-term data center power contracting slows
- **Nuclear operations risk**: Unplanned outages at nuclear facilities are low-probability but high-impact events that can spike costs and reduce contracted revenue

---

## 4. CEG — Constellation Energy
**EV/EBITDA: 14.5x | P/E: 22.3x**

### Thesis Points
- **Largest US nuclear fleet = scarcest clean firm power asset**: Constellation operates ~21 GW of nuclear capacity across 14 sites — an irreplaceable asset base that cannot be replicated given 15–20 year development timelines for new nuclear
- **Microsoft precedent PPA establishes pricing power**: The 20-year Three Mile Island restart deal with Microsoft at ~$100/MWh (vs. ~$35 market at signing) demonstrates hyperscalers will pay substantial premiums for 24/7 carbon-free power; this template is being replicated across the fleet
- **IRA nuclear production tax credits provide earnings floor**: The Inflation Reduction Act's nuclear PTC of ~$15–$43.75/MWh provides a government-backed earnings floor that reduces commodity exposure and de-risks the investment case
- **Data center co-location demand inflects**: IEA projects data centers growing from 3.7% to nearly 12% of US electricity by 2030; Constellation's load-following nuclear is the preferred solution for hyperscalers requiring firm, green power guarantees
- **Valuation discount to replacement cost is extreme**: Building equivalent nuclear capacity today would cost $10,000–$15,000/kW; Constellation's implied enterprise value per kW is a fraction of that

### Key Risks
- **Regulatory/license extension risk**: Nuclear license renewals require NRC approval; adverse decisions could force early plant retirements
- **Political risk to IRA nuclear PTCs**: Legislative rollback of IRA provisions would meaningfully impair earnings and the investment thesis
- **Concentration in a single fuel type**: 100% nuclear exposure means any systematic issue (fuel costs, waste storage regulation) hits the entire fleet simultaneously
- **PPA pricing ceiling**: If wholesale power prices decline sharply (e.g., from massive renewable buildout), Constellation's above-market PPAs become a source of customer renegotiation pressure

---

## 5. ORCL — Oracle Corporation
**EV/EBITDA: 17.1x | P/E: 21.9x**

### Thesis Points
- **OCI (Oracle Cloud Infrastructure) is the fastest-growing major cloud at scale**: OCI is growing revenue 50%+ year-over-year, taking share from AWS/Azure/GCP in specific workloads (database, sovereign cloud, AI training) where Oracle's architecture offers cost/performance advantages
- **AI training cluster business creates a new revenue stream**: Oracle's GPU cluster-as-a-service offering (multi-tenant Nvidia GPU clusters) is winning contracts from AI model developers who need burst compute without hyperscaler commitments; remaining performance obligations grew 62% YoY to ~$130B+
- **Database installed base is the sticky moat**: Oracle's Autonomous Database and Exadata installed base in enterprise creates high switching costs; as enterprises migrate workloads to cloud, Oracle captures the revenue shift rather than losing it
- **Cheapest valuation among cloud infrastructure plays**: At 17x EV/EBITDA, Oracle trades at a substantial discount to MSFT (16x on EBITDA but much slower OCI growth), AMZN (18x), and the semiconductor names — arguably mispricing OCI's growth trajectory
- **Multi-cloud partnerships expand TAM**: OCI interconnect agreements with AWS, Azure, and Google allow Oracle databases to run natively in hyperscaler environments, making Oracle additive to rather than competitive with hyperscaler spend

### Key Risks
- **OCI scale gap remains significant**: AWS at $37.6B/quarter vs. OCI at ~$2.9B/quarter means Oracle must sustain exceptional growth rates for years to become a meaningful infrastructure alternative; execution risk is high
- **Leverage from aggressive data center buildout**: Oracle is spending heavily on OCI data centers; debt levels are elevated and free cash flow conversion is under pressure during the investment phase
- **Core license business secular decline**: Traditional on-premise license revenue continues to shrink; if OCI ramp doesn't offset fast enough, total revenue growth disappoints
- **Competitive intensity**: AWS, Azure, and GCP are all investing to replicate Oracle's database performance advantages within their own managed database services, threatening Oracle's core migration narrative
