# US cybersecurity — primer

## Overview
- The US cybersecurity market reached approximately $82.4 billion in 2024 and is forecast to grow to $86–89 billion in 2025, with further expansion to $120.4 billion by 2030, reflecting a CAGR of roughly 9–12%. — *PureVPN White Label / Market.us Cyber Security Market Report, September 2025*
- Worldwide end-user spending on information security is forecast by Gartner to total $213 billion in 2025, up from $193.4 billion in 2024, with security software alone reaching $105.9 billion — driven primarily by rising AI-enabled threats and GenAI adoption by both defenders and attackers. — *Gartner Newsroom, 'Gartner Forecasts Worldwide End-User Spending on Information Security to Total $213 Billion in 2025,' July 2025*
- The US Generative AI Cybersecurity Market was valued at $1.5 billion in 2024 and is projected to reach $15.0 billion by 2034, registering a CAGR of 28.5%, driven by demand for AI-powered threat detection against increasingly sophisticated attacks. — *Emergen Research, 'US Generative AI Cybersecurity Market Size, Share & Growth Report 2024–2034,' 2024*
- AI-assisted cyberattacks increased 72% year-over-year in 2025; phishing campaigns surged 1,265% since 2023 due to generative AI tools; and 16% of all 2025 breaches involved attackers' use of AI — with AI-powered breaches costing an average of $5.72 million, up 13% from the prior year. — *Total Assure Blog, 'AI Cybersecurity Statistics in 2025: Comprehensive Data on Threats, Detection, and Defense,' citing IBM X-Force and IBM Cost of Breach Report, 2025*
- In the second half of 2024, phishing emails surged 202% and credential phishing attacks rose 703%, with 3-in-5 participants in studies being fooled by AI-automated phishing at success rates comparable to human-generated scams — but at massively greater scale. — *Programs.com, 'New Report: Over 80% of Cyberattacks Now Use AI,' citing SoSafe and Deloitte data, 2025*
- Vishing (AI voice phishing) attacks increased 442% between the first and second halves of 2024; by Q1 2025 vishing accounted for over 60% of all phishing-related incident response engagements; and Deloitte projects generative AI could enable US fraud losses of $40 billion annually by 2027. — *Adaptive Security Blog, 'Multichannel AI Phishing: Email, Voice, SMS & Video,' citing CrowdStrike 2025 Global Threat Report and Cisco Talos Q1 2025 data*
- US data breach costs hit a record high of $10.22 million per incident in 2025 — up 9% year-over-year and nearly 2.3× the global average of $4.44 million — driven by steeper regulatory fines and higher detection and escalation costs; organizations using AI and automation extensively saved nearly $1.9 million per breach on average. — *IBM Cost of a Data Breach Report 2025, as reported by CyberScoop and AllCovered, 2025*
- Enterprises are investing 17 times more in AI-powered security tools ($49 billion in 2025) than in securing the AI infrastructure those tools run on ($2.8 billion), creating a critical governance blind spot; Gartner projects over 75% of enterprises will use AI-amplified cybersecurity products by 2028, up from under 25% in 2025. — *Software Strategies Blog, 'Information Security Spending 2026 Hits $244.2B As Agentic AI Outpaces Defenses 8 to 1,' citing Gartner Forecast: Information Security, Worldwide, 2023–2029, 4Q25, March 2026*
- The global cybersecurity talent shortage stands at 4.8 million unfilled professionals worldwide; in the United States specifically, there is a shortfall of 225,000 mid-level cybersecurity workers, while AI Security (cited by 34% of organizations) and Cloud Security (30%) are the top missing skill sets in 2025. — *ISC2 2025 Cybersecurity Workforce Study; Xantrion citing Lightcast research, 2025*
- 86% of organizations reported at least one breach in 2024, with 28% suffering five or more breaches; over half (52%) say breaches cost them more than $1 million; and 80% say AI tools are helping their security teams, yet the skills shortage of 4.7 million professionals remains unsolved. — *Fortinet 2025 Cybersecurity Skills Gap Global Research Report, citing ISC2 2024 Cybersecurity Workforce Study*
- 2025 set a record for cybersecurity M&A with $74.49 billion across 35 announced deals — nearly 5× the value of 2024 — headlined by Google's $32 billion acquisition of Wiz and Palo Alto Networks' $25 billion acquisition of CyberArk; AI security capabilities were a defining acquisition theme, with AI-security asset spending surpassing $1.6 billion. — *LinkedIn / CRN, '10 Big Cybersecurity Acquisition Deals In 2025,' citing industry M&A data, 2025*
- AI security startup funding grew 8× in two years, from $274 million across 8 deals in Q1 2024 to $2.17 billion across 28 deals in Q4 2025; total AI security startup funding for 2025 reached $6.34 billion, nearly triple the $2.16 billion raised in all of 2024. — *Software Strategies Blog, 'AI Security Market 2025: Funding Data, Top Startups & ServiceNow's Play,' citing Crunchbase data, December 30, 2025*
- President Trump signed a sweeping cybersecurity executive order in June 2025 that refocused US policy to emphasize AI vulnerability management — including incident tracking and indicators of compromise for AI systems — and directed CISA to establish a post-quantum cryptography product list by December 2025, with the Biden administration having previously estimated federal post-quantum transition costs at $7.1 billion through 2035. — *Bank Info Security / CU Info Security, 'Trump Rewrites Cybersecurity Policy in Executive Order,' June 7, 2025*

# US Cybersecurity – AI-Driven Threat Landscape: Investment Shortlist

---

## Screening Rationale

The macro setup favors: **(1)** platforms with AI-native detection/response, **(2)** identity/access management given credential phishing +703% YoY, **(3)** exposure to the talent-shortage tailwind (automation replacing headcount), and **(4)** M&A optionality in a $74B deal year. I screen out names with missing data (CYBR acquired by PANW at $25B; SentinelOne ticker issue) and flag valuation discipline where relevant.

---

## Shortlist: 5 Names

---

### 1. CrowdStrike (CRWD)
**Theme: AI-native EDR/XDR platform; consolidation winner**

| Thesis Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **AI at core, not bolted on** | Falcon platform built on AI from inception; Charlotte AI copilot accelerates SOC response — directly monetizes the 4.8M talent shortage tailwind |
| **Module land-and-expand** | Customers adopting 5+ modules growing fastest; ARPU expansion offsets macro churn risk; platform stickiness creates switching costs |
| **Breach cost savings = ROI story** | IBM data shows AI/automation saves ~$1.9M per breach; CRWD can quantify and sell this directly to CFOs, not just CISOs |
| **Vishing/identity threat surge** | 442% rise in vishing + 703% credential phishing drives demand for CRWD's Identity Protection module — a newer, high-growth segment |
| **M&A target or acquirer** | $207B EV but still a logical consolidator of niche AI security startups ($6.34B funded in 2025); feed the platform flywheel |

**Key Risks:**
- **Valuation stretched**: EV/EBITDA essentially infinite on trailing basis ($59M EBITDA vs. $207B EV); requires flawless execution and continued hyper-growth
- **July 2024 outage overhang**: Reputational damage and litigation costs linger; any repeat incident is existential
- **Competitive intensity**: PANW platformization strategy directly attacks CRWD's consolidation narrative with greater profitability
- **Macro sensitivity**: Enterprise security budgets discretionary at the margin; deal elongation in a slowdown hits CRWD disproportionately

---

### 2. Palo Alto Networks (PANW)
**Theme: Profitability-inflecting platform; best-in-class margin story**

| Thesis Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Platformization creating durable FCF** | "Platformization" strategy converting point-solution customers to multi-product contracts; EBITDA $1.48B and growing — rare positive EBITDA at scale in this group |
| **AI Security Operations (Cortex)** | Cortex XSIAM is an AI-driven SOC platform; directly addresses the 34% of orgs citing AI Security as their #1 missing skill — sells automation as headcount replacement |
| **Post-quantum and federal tailwind** | Trump EO mandates CISA post-quantum product list by Dec 2025; $7.1B federal transition budget; PANW's government segment well-positioned |
| **CyberArk acquisition integration** | $25B CyberArk deal (identity security) is highly strategic given credential phishing explosion; identity + network + cloud = true platform |
| **Margin expansion runway** | Operating leverage as platformization drives higher-margin software mix; path to 30%+ operating margins is credible |

**Key Risks:**
- **Integration execution risk**: CyberArk at $25B is a massive bet; integration complexity could dilute focus and margin
- **Valuation demands perfection**: $288B EV on $1.48B EBITDA (~194x) prices in a very long growth runway
- **Billings volatility**: Platformization involves upfront discounting that pressures near-term billings, confusing growth optics
- **Talent retention post-M&A**: Key CyberArk engineers/sales talent attrition is a real risk post-close

---

### 3. Fortinet (FTNT)
**Theme: Valuation anomaly — profitable, growing, and cheap relative to peers**

| Thesis Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Best EBITDA in the group**: $2.36B EBITDA at $118B EV (~50x) — significantly cheaper than CRWD or PANW on earnings power | Profitability provides downside protection absent from loss-making peers |
| **OT/IoT security leadership** | Operational technology security is underinvested and growing rapidly; FTNT's FortiGate dominance in OT is a defensible moat as industrial AI systems become targets |
| **AI-driven threat detection built-in** | FortiAI integrated across the Security Fabric; benefits from the 72% YoY increase in AI-assisted attacks driving upgrade cycles |
| **SMB and mid-market exposure** | Serves segments underserved by CRWD/PANW; SMBs disproportionately impacted by breach cost inflation ($1M+ for 52% of organizations) — expanding TAM |
| **Hardware refresh cycle** | Appliance installed base entering refresh cycle after 2022–23 digestion; revenue re-acceleration expected as customers upgrade to AI-capable hardware |

**Key Risks:**
- **Hardware model risk**: Appliance-centric model is structurally less favored vs. pure cloud/SaaS; margin mix could deteriorate
- **China exposure**: Manufacturing concentration and potential export restriction complications
- **Slower growth perception**: Profitable but lower growth rate may not capture AI-hype multiple expansion
- **Competitive displacement**: Cloudflare and Zscaler eating into SASE/SD-WAN market from the cloud-native side

---

### 4. Cloudflare (NET)
**Theme: AI-era network security infrastructure; longest runway in the group**

| Thesis Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Network as the security layer** | Cloudflare's global edge network (330+ cities) is uniquely positioned to inspect, filter, and secure AI-generated traffic at scale — no one else has this infrastructure moat |
| **AI Gateway and Workers AI** | First mover in AI traffic security (prompt injection, data exfiltration from LLMs); as enterprises deploy AI agents, NET's AI Gateway becomes mandatory infrastructure |
| **Zero Trust + SASE convergence** | SASE market converging on zero-trust architecture; NET's one-platform approach (no hardware) perfectly aligned with hybrid/remote workforce security needs |
| **Phishing/Bot mitigation at network layer** | 1,265% surge in AI phishing and 202% surge in phishing emails — NET intercepts this at DNS/HTTP layer before it reaches endpoints; positions security as utility |
| **Land-and-expand with developers** | Developer-led adoption creates bottom-up enterprise penetration; R2/Workers ecosystem locks customers into the network fabric over time |

**Key Risks:**
- **Valuation is the biggest risk**: $96B EV on negative EBITDA (-$36M); stock pricing in near-perfection over a decade
- **Commoditization pressure**: AWS, Google, and Akamai all competing on CDN/edge security; pricing power may erode
- **AI infrastructure spend imbalance**: The $49B vs. $2.8B AI security governance gap could close slowly, delaying NET's AI security monetization
- **Path to profitability**: FCF positive but GAAP profitability remains elusive; multiple compression risk in risk-off environments

---

### 5. Qualys (QLYS)
**Theme: Contrarian value play — profitable, cash-generative, M&A target**

| Thesis Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Cheapest profitable name in the group** | $5.2B EV, $245M EBITDA (~21x), EPS of $5.40 — screaming value vs. peers; free cash flow generative with consistent buybacks |
| **Vulnerability management is non-discretionary** | Every AI system deployed creates new attack surface; Trump EO mandates AI vulnerability tracking — QLYS's VMDR platform is directly in scope |
| **AI-powered risk quantification** | TotalCloud and CyberSecurity Asset Management products use AI to prioritize remediation — addresses the talent shortage by automating analyst triage |
| **M&A target in $74B deal environment** | At $5B EV, QLYS is affordable for PANW, CRWD, or a PE buyout; 2025 M&A frenzy with AI security as defining theme makes QLYS a logical target |
| **Recurring, sticky revenue** | SaaS-based platform with ~$500M ARR, high net retention; cloud-delivered model means no hardware refresh risk |

**Key Risks:**
- **Growth rate is modest**: Mid-single-digit revenue growth looks uninspiring vs. hypergrowth peers; may not attract growth-oriented capital
- **Competitive displacement**: Tenable, Rapid7, and CRWD's vulnerability management module all competing directly; market share erosion risk
- **AI platform transformation risk**: Slower pace of AI feature integration vs. better-funded competitors could cause customer churn
- **Small-cap illiquidity**: $5B market cap limits institutional position sizing; can underperform in momentum-driven markets
