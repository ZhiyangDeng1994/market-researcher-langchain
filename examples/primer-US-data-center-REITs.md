# US data center REITs — primer

## Overview
- North American colocation vacancy rates fell to a record low of 1.6% in H1 2025, down from 1.9% in H2 2024 and 2.8% in H1 2024, even as primary market supply grew 43.4% year-over-year to a record 8,155 MW — underscoring that demand is outrunning even record-pace construction. — *CBRE, North America Data Center Trends H1 2025, September 8, 2025*
- Total North American colocation absorption reached 4.4 GW in 2024, a quadruple increase since 2020, with 88% of that absorption concentrated in primary markets. Northern Virginia alone captured 50% of all North American demand in H2 2024 (847 MW). — *JLL, North America Data Center Report, 2025 (via Data Center Dynamics, 2025)*
- More than 2.6 GW of colocation capacity was completed during 2024, and 'almost all of that was absorbed at delivery,' according to JLL. At year-end 2024, 6.6 GW of colocation capacity was still under construction, with 78% in primary markets. — *JLL, via Data Center Dynamics, 2025*
- Data center rents surged 12% year-over-year in 2024, with an 11% CAGR since 2020. Tenants renewing five-year leases are facing up to 50% rent increases, and landlord concessions are becoming increasingly rare in the tight market. — *JLL (Andrew Batson, Head of US Data Center Research), via Data Center Dynamics, 2025*
- Average asking rates for large deployments of 10 MW+ increased by up to 19% in H1 2025, driven by hyperscaler demand, limited power availability, and elevated construction costs. In Northern Virginia specifically, 10 MW+ deal pricing climbed 13.8% since year-end 2024. — *CBRE, North America Data Center Trends H1 2025, September 8, 2025*
- 74.3% of all under-construction capacity across primary US markets in H1 2025 was already pre-leased, driven by cloud and AI providers locking in future space amid power and land constraints. Preleasing now extends to capacity slated for delivery in 2027 and beyond in Northern Virginia. — *CBRE, North America Data Center Trends H1 2025 / CBRE Northern Virginia Market Profile, September 8, 2025*
- The North America data center vacancy rate stood at an unprecedented 2.3% as of mid-2025, per JLL's midyear report, with inventory growing to a record 15.5 GW. Markets like Northern Virginia reported vacancy below 0.72% and Dallas/Fort Worth below 1%, indicating effectively zero available capacity. — *JLL, North America Data Center Report – Midyear 2025, August 18, 2025*
- US data center construction under construction fell 5.7% in 2025 to 5.99 GW from 6.35 GW — the first contraction in active US construction since 2020 — even as hyperscaler capex approaches $700 billion for 2026 alone. The bottleneck has shifted from capital to execution, with power transformer lead times stretching 128–144 weeks. — *Introl, citing CBRE Group data, 2025/2026*
- Approximately 600 GW of US data center projects are still seeking power capacity, while only 183 GW have secured construction or electricity supply agreements with utilities, according to Wood Mackenzie's US data center pipeline analysis. — *Wood Mackenzie, US Data Center Pipeline Report, via SaurEnergy, 2025*
- US data center grid power demand is projected by S&P Global to grow 22% in 2025 to 61.8 GW, and nearly triple to 134.4 GW by 2030, with Virginia's demand alone rising from 9.3 GW in 2024 to 12.1 GW in 2025. The average wait time for US data center grid interconnection is now approximately four years. — *S&P Global forecast, via LinkedIn/JLL analysis, 2025*
- Data center REITs produced a 77% total return in the five years ending December 31, 2024 (+12% annualized), versus +24% total return for the MSCI US REIT Index (+4% annualized) over the same period. Equinix and Digital Realty returned +19% and +36% respectively in 2024 alone. — *Chilton Capital Management, Data Center REITs: Own the Real Estate Behind AI, August 1, 2025*
- The US data center colocation market (by investment) was valued at $43.71 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach $85.18 billion by 2031, growing at an 11.76% CAGR, with cumulative investment of ~$461.62 billion expected during 2026–2031. — *Arizton, US Data Center Colocation Market Size & Share Report, 2025/2026*
- Iron Mountain's data center segment reported 26% organic storage revenue growth year-over-year and strong renewal pricing of +13% (cash) and +20% (GAAP) in Q2 2025, with the company planning to nearly triple its capacity to ~1.3 GW. It expects at least 25% data center revenue growth in 2026 from existing backlog alone. — *Iron Mountain, Q2 2025 Earnings Presentation, 2025*
- Global data center capacity is projected by JLL to expand at a 14% CAGR through 2030, adding nearly 100 GW of new capacity between 2026 and 2030 to potentially reach 200 GW globally. The Americas — led overwhelmingly by the US — holds ~50% of global capacity and is forecast to grow at the fastest pace with a 17% supply CAGR. — *JLL, 2026 Global Data Center Market Outlook, 2025/2026*
- In Q3 2025, the data center REIT sector recorded approximately 21% year-over-year FFO growth, and the data center physical infrastructure market grew ~18% YoY in Q3 2025, with the top 10 cloud providers growing more than 30% globally and posting even stronger gains in North America. — *Aranca, Digital Infrastructure REITs Special Report, citing Bloomberg data as of January 16, 2026*

# US Data Center REITs — Shortlist

## Sector Setup
Record-low vacancy (1.6–2.3%), 74% pre-leased pipelines, +12–19% rent growth, and a construction bottleneck (power transformer lead times 128–144 weeks) create a multi-year landlord pricing cycle. Demand is structural (AI/cloud capex ~$700B in 2026); supply is physically constrained. The question is which names capture the most rent upside with the least execution risk.  `[UNSOURCED]`

---

## 1. Digital Realty Trust (DLR) — *Primary Long*

**EV/EBITDA: ~28x | Price: $176**

### Thesis Points
1. **Scale + geography**: DLR owns ~300 data centers across 25+ countries; its global interconnection fabric makes it the natural home for hyperscaler multi-region deployments — the fastest-growing demand pocket.
2. **Lease-up torque**: With vacancy sub-2% across primary markets, DLR's existing stabilized assets are repricing on renewal at 40–50% cash bumps; every lease roll is accretive without a single shovel in the ground.
3. **Development pipeline discipline**: 6.6 GW still under construction industrywide, but DLR's pre-leased development (aligned with the 74% sector pre-lease rate) means capex is matched to contracted revenue — limiting speculative risk.
4. **FFO re-rating**: Sector FFO grew ~21% YoY in Q3 2025; DLR's mix of stabilized + lease-up assets positions it to grow FFO above that pace as legacy below-market leases roll to spot.
5. **Power optionality**: DLR's established utility relationships in key markets (Northern Virginia, Chicago, Frankfurt) give it queue priority versus greenfield entrants facing 4-year interconnection waits.

### Key Risks
- **Hyperscaler concentration**: Top 5 tenants likely >40% of revenue; any single customer pullback creates material occupancy risk.
- **Balance sheet leverage**: REITs carry structural leverage; rising long rates compress FFO multiples and increase refinancing cost.
- **Northern Virginia saturation**: 50% of North American demand in one market is also 50% of the risk if Virginia utility constraints tighten further.

---

## 2. Equinix (EQIX) — *Core Long / Quality Compounder*

**EV/EBITDA: ~29x | Price: $1,023**

### Thesis Points
1. **Interconnection moat**: EQIX's ~250 IBX campuses are the industry's neutral interconnection hubs — a structural switching-cost business that commands premium pricing unrelated to commodity colocation cycles.
2. **xScale JV capital recycling**: Joint ventures with sovereign wealth funds (GIC, CPPIB) allow EQIX to develop hyperscaler-grade capacity off-balance-sheet while retaining management fees and interconnection revenue — high-return, asset-light growth.
3. **Pricing power in retail colo**: Retail colocation (enterprise, financial services, media) faces even tighter supply than wholesale; EQIX's premium positioning insulates it from the most commoditized pricing dynamics.
4. **International diversification**: ~50% of revenue is non-US, providing natural hedge against Northern Virginia-specific power/regulatory risk and access to undersupplied EMEA/APAC markets.
5. **Dividend growth + re-rating**: 11% CAGR in data center REIT returns since 2020; EQIX's consistent FFO/dividend growth supports multiple expansion as AI infrastructure becomes a "must-own" allocation.

### Key Risks
- **Premium valuation**: At ~29x EV/EBITDA, any guidance miss or macro rate spike compresses the multiple sharply — limited margin of safety.
- **xScale execution**: JV structures add complexity; if hyperscaler demand softens, committed development capital creates stranded cost risk.
- **Regulatory/data sovereignty**: European data localization rules and tax authority scrutiny (EQIX lost REIT status challenge in Germany) add jurisdictional risk.

---

## 3. Iron Mountain (IRM) — *Asymmetric Compounder*

**EV/EBITDA: ~23x | Price: $124**

### Thesis Points
1. **Underappreciated data center growth**: Data center segment growing 26% organically; market still partially prices IRM as a physical records storage company — creating a valuation gap versus pure-play peers.
2. **Capacity tripling from low base**: Plans to scale from ~500 MW to ~1.3 GW; early-stage capacity growth delivers exponential FFO uplift as fixed costs are spread over a larger revenue base.
3. **Backlog visibility**: Management guides at least 25% data center revenue growth in 2026 from *existing signed backlog alone* — not dependent on new lease-up.
4. **Renewal pricing momentum**: +13% cash / +20% GAAP renewal spreads in Q2 2025, with room to accelerate as the market tightens further.
5. **Balance sheet transformation**: Records storage generates stable, high-margin cash flows that fund data center development — lower cost of capital than a pure-play developer.

### Key Risks
- **Execution risk of tripling capacity**: Going from ~500 MW to 1.3 GW requires flawless power procurement, construction management, and leasing — any slip delays FFO realization.
- **Blended multiple discount**: The market may persistently apply a conglomerate discount until data centers are a clear majority of EBITDA (~2027–2028 timeframe).
- **Legacy business secular decline**: Physical records storage is a melting ice cube; if digital migration accelerates faster than modeled, the "funding engine" weakens.

---

## 4. Vertiv Holdings (VRT) — *Infrastructure Enabler / Picks-and-Shovels*

**EV/EBITDA: ~49x | Price: $305**

### Thesis Points
1. **Unavoidable bottleneck exposure**: VRT makes power distribution, thermal management, and prefabricated modular systems — the exact equipment with 128–144 week lead times. Backlog is structurally locked in.
2. **Pricing power from scarcity**: Component shortages mean VRT can pass through cost inflation and expand margins; the company has demonstrated consistent ASP increases with minimal pushback.
3. **AI density tailwind**: Higher-compute AI servers (GB200 NVL rack configurations) require 10–100x more power management sophistication per rack — expanding VRT's content per data center dollar spent even if MW growth slows.
4. **Order book visibility**: Long lead times convert to multi-year revenue visibility; book-to-bill above 1x provides earnings predictability unusual in industrial companies.
5. **Global manufacturing scale**: VRT's global footprint (Americas, EMEA, APAC) allows it to serve data center buildouts wherever they occur — not constrained to US power availability.

### Key Risks
- **Valuation is stretched**: ~49x EV/EBITDA embeds significant perfection; any order slowdown, margin miss, or hyperscaler capex pause triggers sharp de-rating.
- **Customer concentration**: Hyperscalers (Google, Microsoft, Meta, Amazon) likely represent a dominant share of backlog — a single large customer delay ripples through the P&L.
- **Competition from ODMs**: White-box/direct manufacturers and new entrants (Delta, Huawei internationally) are targeting VRT's core market.

---

## 5. CoreWeave / Smaller Operators — *Pass for Now*

Private or subscale operators lack the power queues, balance sheets, and pre-leasing relationships to compete in the current environment. CONE (CyrusOne), QTS, and SWCH have all been taken private — validating the thesis but removing the public float. No actionable public small-cap pure-play meets the quality threshold.
