# US defense and aerospace — primer

## Overview
- The Biden administration's FY2025 DoD budget request totaled $849.8 billion, with $143.2 billion allocated for Research, Development, Test & Evaluation (RDT&E) — up from $139.7 billion in FY2024. The FY2025 budget operated under the Fiscal Responsibility Act of 2023 national defense spending cap of $895 billion. — *Digitimes, March 13, 2024; The World Data, 2025*
- The Trump administration's FY2026 DoD budget request totaled $961.6 billion — comprising $848.3 billion in discretionary funding plus $113.3 billion in mandatory reconciliation funding. This represents approximately a 13% increase from prior enacted levels and is widely described as the first near-$1 trillion defense budget. — *EveryCRSReport.com (Congressional Research Service), FY2026 Defense Budget report; Overt Defense, July 2025*
- When including the $157 billion in FY2025 reconciliation law funds (P.L. 119-21, the 'One Big Beautiful Bill Act'), the total FY2025 DoD budget topped $1.055 trillion, combining $899 billion in annual congressional appropriations and $156.2 billion in reconciliation funds, according to analyst Stephen Semler. — *Project On Government Oversight (POGO), July 2025*
- The FY2026 DoD budget allocated $295.3 billion for weapon system accounts: $153.3 billion for procurement and $142 billion for RDT&E. The Pentagon's FY2026 proposed R&D budget rose to $179 billion total, targeting hypersonics, AI, drone swarms, and advanced autonomy. — *EveryCRSReport.com / Congressional Research Service; Task & Purpose, July 16, 2025*
- Congress passed an FY2026 defense appropriations package of $839 billion — $8 billion more than the Pentagon's base budget request — including $13.4 billion to augment the Golden Dome missile defense system and $4 billion for missile warning and tracking satellites. — *Air & Space Forces Magazine, 2025*
- The Golden Dome missile defense initiative received a $24.4 billion down payment via the FY2025 reconciliation law. President Trump stated the full system will cost $175 billion; the Congressional Budget Office estimated a limited space-based interceptor variant alone could exceed $500 billion. — *Congressional Research Service Defense Primer: The Golden Dome for America; Kal Capital Markets, July 7, 2025*
- The Space Force budget surpassed $40 billion in FY2026 when reconciliation funds are included — roughly a 30% year-over-year increase from FY2025 enacted levels — covering satellite resilience, space domain awareness, and orbital defense. — *SpacePolicyOnline.com, 2025; Solomon Partners US Defense Technology Priorities, October 2025*
- In FY2024, DoD obligated $445 billion in defense contracts — more than all other U.S. government agencies combined ($310 billion). The top five contractors — Lockheed Martin, RTX, Boeing, Northrop Grumman, and General Dynamics — received 30% of all departmental contract obligations. — *Congressional Research Service, Defense Primer: Department of Defense Contractors (Congress.gov)*
- Lockheed Martin was the #1 DoD prime contractor in FY2024 with $50.7 billion in contract awards. RTX ranked #2 at $24.8 billion, Boeing #3 at $23.2 billion, General Dynamics #4 at $19.2 billion, and Northrop Grumman #5 at $18.6 billion. Emerging tech firms like SpaceX rose to #28 and Anduril debuted at #74. — *Defense Security Monitor / Forecast International, Top 100 Defense Contractors 2024, November 2025*
- RTX Corp reported a $236 billion order backlog as of Q2 2025, reflecting 15% year-on-year growth. Q2 2025 revenue was $21.6 billion, up 9% year-over-year, driven by strong defense electronics and missile systems demand. — *Yahoo Finance / GuruFocus RTX Corp Analysis, 2025*
- U.S. total military equipment sales to foreign governments reached a record $318.7 billion in FY2024 — a 29% increase year-over-year — comprising $117.9 billion in Foreign Military Sales (FMS, up 45.7% from FY2023) and $200.8 billion in Direct Commercial Sales (up 27.5%). — *Army Technology / U.S. State Department data, January 27, 2025; HLC Publications, 2025*
- The global aerospace and defense market was valued at approximately $846.9 billion in 2025 and is projected to grow to $1.47 trillion by 2032, at a CAGR of 8.2%, driven by commercial aviation fleet renewal and defense modernization fueled by geopolitical tensions. — *Dynamix / CFAW Aerospace, Defense and Government Industry News, Fall 2025 (citing Coherent Market Insights)*
- The global UAV market is expected to grow from $36 billion in 2024 to $41 billion in 2025 and reach $200 billion by 2035, with a 16% CAGR between 2024 and 2037. The North American market alone is forecast to reach ~$100 billion by 2037, with defense spending accounting for roughly a quarter of that total. — *UHY Advisors, 2025 Aerospace Industry Market Analysis*
- Global defense spending surpassed $2.2 trillion in 2024, with the U.S., China, and NATO countries leading investments in hypersonics, cyber warfare, AI, and space-based systems. The U.S. accounted for the largest single share, with SIPRI reporting U.S. military expenditure at $954 billion in 2025. — *UHY Advisors, 2025 Aerospace Industry Market Analysis; Eurasian Times citing SIPRI, 2025*
- The FY2026 defense budget allocated over $15 billion for cybersecurity and cyberspace operations, $10 billion+ for hypersonic and long-range strike systems, and $20.3 billion for Science & Technology programs (an 8% increase), with new standalone budget lines for quantum computing and post-quantum cryptography. — *Solomon Partners, U.S. Defense Technology Priorities, October 2025*

## Shortlist: 5 Names

---

### 1. NOC — Northrop Grumman
**Theme:** B-21 Raider + Space Force + Golden Dome beneficiary at a discount to peers

**Thesis Points:**
- 12.3x EV/EBITDA is the cheapest among large-cap primes — ~35% discount to RTX — despite arguably the highest-quality program mix (B-21, GBSD/Sentinel ICBM, Space Force satellites)  `[UNSOURCED]`
- Golden Dome's $24.4B down payment and $175B+ total cost estimate directly targets NOC's missile defense and space-based sensor architecture capabilities (HALO satellite program, GEM-T)
- Space Force budget surging ~30% YoY to $40B+; NOC's space segment (satellites, strategic deterrence) is the most levered large-cap play on this line item  `[UNSOURCED]`
- B-21 Raider at low-rate initial production — cost-plus development losses are burning off; as the program scales to full-rate production, margin recovery is a multi-year earnings catalyst
- FMS tailwinds: allied nations rapidly modernizing strategic deterrence post-Ukraine; NOC's classified programs and restricted-sale technologies insulate it from most export competition

**Key Risks:**
- B-21 remains in cost-plus phase; any further cost overruns could pressure margins and generate negative headlines
- GBSD/Sentinel ICBM program risk — large fixed-price elements introduced; cost growth could compress returns
- Concentration in slower-moving strategic programs means less near-term revenue velocity vs. missile/munitions peers
- Any administration pivot away from nuclear modernization or Golden Dome scale-back hits NOC disproportionately

---

### 2. RTX — RTX Corp
**Theme:** The missile economy's toll booth — unmatched backlog, production-constrained not demand-constrained

**Thesis Points:**
- $236B backlog (15% YoY growth) is the largest in the sector and provides ~5+ years of revenue visibility; Q2 2025 revenue of $21.6B (+9% YoY) demonstrates conversion is accelerating  `[UNSOURCED]`
- Raytheon Missiles & Defense is structurally irreplaceable in the near term: Patriot/PAC-3, StormBreaker, AIM-120, Tomahawk — nearly every high-demand munition flowing to Ukraine, Taiwan deterrence, and Middle East allies runs through RTX
- FY2026 RDT&E budget of $142B+ and hypersonics/long-range strike allocation of $10B+ directly funds RTX's next-generation programs (hypersonic glide body, directed energy)
- Pratt & Whitney commercial engine cycle (GTF) creates a natural hedge — commercial aviation recovery provides EBITDA floor even in a defense spending plateau scenario
- Record FMS of $117.9B in FY2024 (+45.7% YoY) — RTX is the primary beneficiary as Patriot batteries and advanced missiles dominate allied procurement lists  `[UNSOURCED]`

**Key Risks:**
- 19.5x EV/EBITDA is the richest multiple among large-caps; execution must remain near-flawless to justify premium  `[UNSOURCED]`
- GTF powder metal disk issue — ongoing engine inspections and shop visits remain a cash drag and reputational overhang on the Collins/P&W segment
- Production capacity is the binding constraint, not demand; supply chain bottlenecks (energetics, microelectronics) could cause backlog conversion to disappoint
- Any Ukraine ceasefire or Middle East de-escalation reduces the acute pull-through demand narrative, even if structural demand remains

---

### 3. KTOS — Kratos Defense & Security Solutions
**Theme:** Pure-play attritable drone and autonomous systems — the highest-beta expression of the Pentagon's pivot to mass over exquisite

**Thesis Points:**
- DoD's explicit shift toward low-cost attritable drones (XQ-58 Valkyrie, tactical drone swarms) plays directly to Kratos's core competency — the company is arguably the only publicly traded pure-play in affordable, expendable unmanned systems
- UAV market growing at 16% CAGR from $36B (2024) toward $200B by 2035; Kratos's first-mover advantage in jet-powered tactical drones creates IP and manufacturing moat that larger primes cannot easily replicate quickly  `[UNSOURCED]`
- FY2026 RDT&E budget targeted $20.3B for S&T programs plus explicit drone swarm and advanced autonomy allocations — KTOS is on contract for multiple classified and unclassified programs in this pipeline
- Anduril's debut at #74 on contractor rankings validates the "new defense tech" category; Kratos is further along the revenue curve ($1B+ revenues) and already embedded in legacy DoD acquisition workflows, giving it a regulatory moat vs. pure SV startups
- Small EV ($8B) means a single large program award (e.g., Collaborative Combat Aircraft selection, loyal wingman contract) can be a step-change re-rating event

**Key Risks:**
- 99x+ EV/EBITDA — essentially a growth/optionality valuation; any program delay or loss converts immediately to a sharp de-rating  `[UNSOURCED]`
- Margin structure is thin (EBITDA of $81M on ~$1B revenue); path to meaningful free cash flow requires substantial volume scaling
- Faces competition from Boeing's MQ-28, General Atomics, and potentially Anduril for major contract awards — program concentration risk is high
- A procurement pause or CR (continuing resolution) environment disproportionately harms newer, smaller contractors whose programs lack mature funding lines

---

### 4. LDOS — Leidos Holdings
**Theme:** The cheapest defense name on multiples — IT modernization, classified services, and DOGE-resilient revenue mix

**Thesis Points:**
- 8.4x EV/EBITDA is the lowest among all names screened (ex-BAH); at $108/share the market is pricing significant headwinds that appear overstated given Leidos's contract diversification  `[UNSOURCED]`
- Defense IT modernization is a stated FY2026 priority (cybersecurity allocated $15B+); Leidos is the #1 IT contractor to the DoD and intelligence community, directly in the critical path of this spending
- Revenue mix skews toward cost-reimbursable classified services — less exposed to fixed-price manufacturing risk that is hurting Boeing and early NOC B-21 margins
- DOGE concerns are better directed at BAH/CACI (advisory/consulting) than Leidos — Leidos operates mission-critical systems (DHMSM military health records, airspace management, nuclear command-and-control support) that are non-discretionary
- Potential re-rating catalyst: the market has grouped all government services names together under DOGE fears; as contract wins normalize and the distinction between discretionary consulting and mission-critical IT becomes clearer, multiple expansion from 8.4x toward the sector's 12-15x range implies 40-75% upside  `[UNSOURCED]`

**Key Risks:**
- DOGE-driven contract review could still result in scope reductions or cancellations across civilian agency work (VA, DHS) which represents a meaningful portion of revenue
- Low multiple may be structurally justified if growth decelerates — Leidos has lower organic growth potential than pure defense hardware players
- Re-compete risk on large single-award contracts (DHMSM, GSM-O II) — losing a major re-compete would be a significant earnings event
- Less direct exposure to the highest-growth budget lines (munitions, space, hypersonics) means it may lag peers in a strong defense upcycle

---

### 5. HII — Huntington Ingalls Industries
**Theme:** Monopoly shipbuilder at trough valuation — Navy recapitalization is a decade-long must-do, not a discretionary program

**Thesis Points:**
- 12.0x EV/EBITDA at the lowest absolute EV ($13.7B) among names screened — the market is pricing in near-permanent margin compression and program struggles that are partially cyclical, not structural  `[UNSOURCED]`
- HII is a strategic monopoly: it is the sole builder of U.S. nuclear-powered aircraft carriers (Newport News) and one of only two submarine builders (alongside GD/Electric Boat); the Navy cannot diversify away from HII on its most critical platforms
- Navy shipbuilding is structurally underfunded relative to the 355-ship force goal and China's rapidly expanding fleet — bipartisan political pressure to accelerate procurement makes multi-decade backlog expansion highly probable
- FY2026 reconciliation and base budget include significant naval modernization funding; any acceleration in Virginia-class submarine procurement rate (currently 1.2/year vs. 2/year goal) is a direct HII revenue catalyst
- Dividend yield and capital return: HII's compressed valuation creates a relatively attractive yield profile while investors wait for margin recovery on fixed-price shipbuilding contracts currently in loss positions

**Key Risks:**
- Shipbuilding margins are deeply pressured by labor inflation and productivity challenges at Newport News — fixed-price contracts signed in 2020-2022 are underwater and will drag results through 2026-2027
- Workforce and skilled labor shortage is a genuine operational constraint; shipbuilding is not easily automated and training pipelines are multi-year
- Program execution risk on CVN-82 (Enterprise carrier) and Block V Virginia-class — any additional cost growth triggers negative EAC adjustments
- If the Navy accepts a smaller fleet goal or extends carrier service lives, the demand cadence for new hulls could soften, leaving HII with high fixed costs and insufficient volume
