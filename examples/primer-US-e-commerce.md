# US e-commerce — primer

## Overview
- The US e-commerce logistics market is projected at USD 150.86 billion in 2025 and expected to reach USD 236.85 billion by 2031, growing at a CAGR of 7.71%. Transportation accounts for 66.5% of the market's share, while warehousing and fulfillment is forecast to expand at a 7.9% CAGR through 2031. — *Mordor Intelligence, 'US E-commerce Logistics Market – Size, Share & Industry Analysis', 2025*
- US e-commerce sales hit a record high of approximately $1.19 trillion in 2024 and are expected to grow by 7.1% annually to reach $1.82 trillion by end of 2025. The market achieved a CAGR of 8.6% during 2020–2024. — *Research and Markets / Yahoo Finance, 'United States B2C Ecommerce Business Report 2025', January 2026*
- Last-mile delivery accounts for 50–53% of total shipping costs, making it the single largest logistics expense for e-commerce brands. Failed deliveries cost retailers an average of $17.20 per attempt. The global last-mile delivery market reached $161.20 billion in 2024 and surged to an estimated $184.2 billion in 2025. — *Locus, 'Last Mile in E-Commerce: Definition, Importance & Benefits', 2025*
- Both UPS and FedEx implemented a 5.9% average General Rate Increase (GRI) effective late December 2024 / January 2025. Key surcharges jumped far more sharply: FedEx Additional Handling charges rose 26–28%, Oversize Package fees increased up to 28%, and UPS Large Package surcharges rose 24–27%. — *Pitney Bowes, 'UPS 2025 Shipping Rates Increase & Charts' and 'FedEx 2025 General Rate Increase Guide', 2025*
- UPS Large Package surcharges for Zones 7 and above increased by 29–34.5% since December 2024, and the Over Maximum Surcharge jumped 42% to $1,775 per package. Amazon also added a 3.5% fuel surcharge to all FBA fulfillment fees starting April 2025. — *FreightWise, '2026 Parcel Shipping News & Rate Updates for FedEx, UPS and Regionals', 2025; AMZ Prep, 'Amazon FBA Fees 2026: Full Breakdown + April 17 Surcharge Update', 2025*
- Amazon FBA fulfillment fees increased an average of 5.2% in 2024, with fees now representing 15–35% of selling price depending on product size and category. Standard FBA fulfillment fees for items priced $10+ range from $3.06 per unit (small standard) to over $203 per unit (extra-large). — *ShipBob / Jungle Scout, 'Calculate Full Amazon FBA Fees & Costs for Brands [2026]', 2025; Adverio.io, 'Amazon FBA Fee Breakdown 2026', 2025*
- Fulfillment typically consumes 5–15% of e-commerce sales revenue, and total logistics costs now account for 12–20% of revenue, projected to rise to 15–25% due to last-mile inflation. Total US logistics costs are projected at approximately $2.6 trillion in 2024, a surge of nearly $1 trillion in five years. — *Ship To The Moon, '25 Key U.S. E-commerce Statistics for 2025', 2025*
- Warehouse labor costs account for 45–57% of total fulfillment operating costs. Average warehouse worker wages reached $16.95 per hour in 2024 (up from $15.78 in 2023), with projections of $17.14 per hour in 2025. Coastal metropolitan area warehouses pay $22–$25 per hour, significantly above the national average. — *Opensend, '27 Warehouse Labor Cost Statistics for eCommerce Stores', 2024–2025*
- The warehousing sector faces a 43% annual workforce turnover rate, and over 40% of warehouse operators report forgoing revenue due to insufficient staffing. The US trucking sector also faces a persistent shortage estimated at 60,000–80,000 drivers in 2025. — *Opensend, '27 Warehouse Labor Cost Statistics for eCommerce Stores', 2024–2025; Market Data Forecast, 'U.S. Warehouse Market Size, Share & Trends, 2034', 2025*
- US retail merchandise returns totaled approximately $890 billion in 2024, representing a 16.9% return rate — roughly double the 8.1% rate recorded in 2019. The average e-commerce return rate was approximately 20.4% in 2024, with apparel reaching 30–40%. Fraudulent and abusive returns accounted for roughly $101–$103 billion. — *National Retail Federation (NRF) via Red Stag Fulfillment, 'What's The Average Ecommerce Return Rate?', 2024; Jay Group, 'ECommerce Returns: Taming The Margin-Eating Force', 2025*
- The global e-commerce fulfillment services market is expected to grow from USD 141.35 billion in 2025 to USD 468.44 billion by 2034, at a CAGR of 14.24%. Third-party logistics (3PL) monthly minimums rose sharply, from $337.50 in 2024 to $517 in 2025, with many providers requiring $500–$2,000 per month to guarantee capacity. — *Precedence Research, 'E-Commerce Fulfillment Services Market Size to Hit USD 468.44 Bn by 2034', 2025; Olimp Warehousing, '2026 Cost Guide: Amazon FBA vs 3PL in the U.S.', 2025*
- 80% of US consumers now expect a same-day delivery option, and 92% of shoppers report that free shipping influences their purchase decisions — up from 83% the prior year. More than 90% of shoppers will abandon their carts when confronted with high shipping costs. — *SmartRoutes, 'Last-Mile Delivery Statistics: The Complete Data Resource for 2026', citing Capital One Shopping (2025), AlixPartners (2024), and McKinsey (2024)*
- Amazon delivered over 8 billion items to US Prime members the same or next day in 2025 — a 30%+ increase over the prior year — and 13 billion items same or next day globally. US Prime members saved an average of $550 on fast, free delivery in 2025, nearly four times the annual membership cost. — *Amazon Newsroom, 'Amazon delivered 13 billion items same or next day in 2025', AboutAmazon.com, 2025*
- The global logistics robotics market was valued at $10.21 billion in 2024 and is projected to reach $44.56 billion by 2034 (CAGR of 15.88%), driven by e-commerce demand, labor shortages, and government support for automation. The broader logistics automation market was valued at $78 billion in 2024 and is expected to reach $88 billion in 2025. — *Precedence Research, 'Logistics Robotics Market Size to Cross USD 44.56 Billion by 2034', June 2025; Infosys, 'Logistics Industry Outlook 2025'*
- Free shipping is the top purchase motivator for 76% of US shoppers in 2025 (up 5% from 2024 and 12% from 2023), while fast shipping (two days or less) dropped to just 15% as a priority — down 5% from 2024. Additionally, 33% of shoppers reported shopping in-store in 2024 specifically to avoid shipping fees. — *Ryder System, 'Ryder 2025 E-commerce Study: 94% of Shoppers Prioritize Cost Savings', Ryder Newsroom, 2025*

## Ideas
# US E-Commerce Logistics & Fulfillment Cost — Idea Shortlist

---

## Framing the Opportunity

The sector is caught in a structural squeeze: e-commerce GMV growing at ~7–8% CAGR while logistics costs inflate at 2–3x that rate (last-mile +50–53% of shipping cost, UPS/FedEx GRIs of 5.9% headline but 24–42% on key surcharges, FBA fees up 5.2%). Winners are those who *own the cost stack* or *arbitrage it*; losers are those who pass it through imperfectly or absorb it.

---

## 1. UPS — **Long (Contrarian Recovery)**

*EV $118.9B | EV/EBITDA ~10.0x | P/E 18.4x*

**Thesis Points**
1. **Trough-earnings setup**: UPS deliberately shed low-margin Amazon volume (2023–24); base is now cleaner — any volume inflection from SMB/healthcare mix improvement drops to EBITDA at high incremental margins.
2. **GRI pricing power is real**: 5.9% headline GRI plus 24–42% surcharge hikes on large/oversize parcels structurally widens revenue-per-piece even on flat volume.
3. **10x EV/EBITDA is at the low end of its 5-year range** (~11–13x normalized); mean reversion alone implies 10–30% upside before operational improvement.
4. **Healthcare/B2B mix shift**: UPS Healthcare (cold chain, Marken) is a structurally higher-margin, less price-elastic segment growing double digits.
5. **Capital return**: $5.5B+ buyback authorization; dividend yield ~5.5% provides downside cushion.

**Key Risks**
- Volume never recovers if macro softens or Amazon builds out delivery network faster than expected.
- Labor cost (Teamsters contract) is largely locked in at elevated rates — operating leverage cuts both ways.
- Secular modal shift: more shippers multi-sourcing to USPS/regionals permanently.

---

## 2. AMZN — **Long (Fulfillment Infrastructure Flywheel)**

*EV $2.84T | EV/EBITDA ~18.2x | P/E 29.6x*

**Thesis Points**
1. **Logistics is becoming a profit center, not a cost center**: Amazon Logistics now handles ~75% of its own US volume; 8B+ same/next-day deliveries in 2025 at cost curves no 3PL can match — increasingly monetized via Seller Fulfilled Prime and Buy with Prime.
2. **FBA fee hikes are margin-accretive with low elasticity**: April 2025 3.5% fuel surcharge layered on top of 5.2% 2024 hikes; third-party sellers have limited alternatives at scale — captive monetization of the cost squeeze.
3. **AWS funds the logistics arms race**: ~$100B+ capex guided for 2025; competitors (UPS, FedEx) cannot match same-day network density investment.
4. **Advertising + fulfillment bundling**: Sellers buying ads are also buying FBA — blended monetization per seller rising even as individual fee lines are contested.
5. **Return infrastructure moat**: Amazon's reverse logistics (returnless refunds, liquidation network) reduces the $17.20/failed-delivery drag that hurts competitors.

**Key Risks**
- Antitrust/regulatory risk on FBA tying arrangements (FTC scrutiny ongoing).
- $100B+ annual capex creates execution risk; any demand air pocket destroys ROIC narrative.
- Tariff/trade policy disruption to third-party seller GMV (Chinese seller concentration).

---

## 3. GXO Logistics — **Long (Pure-Play Automation Beneficiary)**

*EV $11.2B | EV/EBITDA ~11.9x | P/E 44.3x*

**Thesis Points**
1. **Pure-play contract logistics is the cleanest way to own warehouse automation adoption**: GXO deploys robotics/automation for blue-chip customers, passing capex risk to clients while capturing labor-cost-savings upside in margin structure.
2. **Labor cost inflation is a *tailwind* for GXO's value proposition**: At $16.95/hr average warehouse wages (up 7.4% YoY) with 43% turnover, every dollar of wage inflation accelerates customer ROI on automation contracts.
3. **Outsourcing wave is early-cycle**: Over 40% of warehouse operators forgo revenue due to staffing shortfalls — structural demand for GXO's managed services model.
4. **Logistics robotics market growing at 15.9% CAGR to $44.6B by 2034**: GXO is positioned at the intersection of this and the $468B e-commerce fulfillment market.
5. **M&A catalyst**: GXO has been a rumored target; post-spin from XPO it trades at a discount to private market comps for contract logistics assets.

**Key Risks**
- Customer concentration (top 10 customers ~50%+ of revenue) — loss of one anchor contract is severe.  `[UNSOURCED]`
- Long-dated fixed contracts mean GXO absorbs inflation if energy/labor exceeds contract escalators.
- Balance sheet leverage limits flexibility; integration of Wincanton acquisition adds execution risk.

---

## 4. SHOP (Shopify) — **Long (Logistics Cost Arbitrage Platform)**

*EV $154.8B | EV/EBITDA ~72x | P/E 123x*

**Thesis Points**
1. **Shopify Fulfillment Network (SFN) / Flexport partnership aggregates SMB shipping volume** to negotiate carrier rates that no individual merchant can access — directly monetizing the 5.9%+ GRI environment as a differentiator vs. merchants going direct.
2. **"Shop Pay + Fulfillment" bundling raises switching costs**: Merchants using both payments and fulfillment churn at dramatically lower rates; ARPU expansion runway is significant.
3. **Free shipping expectation (92% of shoppers) creates existential pressure on SMB merchants** — they *must* use a platform with rate leverage or margin compression forces them off standalone solutions.
4. **Offline/POS growth diversifies away from pure e-commerce logistics risk**: Omnichannel capabilities reduce sensitivity to any single logistics cost vector.
5. **Operator leverage**: Shopify exited its own fulfillment capex (sold warehouses to Flexport), keeping asset-light model while retaining the merchant relationship — superior ROIC profile vs. owning iron.

**Key Risks**
- Valuation is unforgiving (72x EV/EBITDA); any growth deceleration re-rates violently.  `[UNSOURCED]`
- Amazon's "Buy with Prime" is a direct competitive threat to Shopify's checkout + fulfillment bundle.
- Flexport partnership execution risk — Shopify does not control the physical network.

---

## 5. UBER — **Long (Last-Mile Optionality, Underappreciated)**

*EV $155.3B | EV/EBITDA ~22.1x | P/E 17.9x*

**Thesis Points**
1. **Uber Eats / Uber Freight positions Uber at the highest-cost, highest-growth node of logistics**: Last-mile is 50–53% of total shipping cost and growing fastest — Uber's gig model is structurally cheaper than UPS/FedEx for density-constrained urban same-day.
2. **80% of consumers now expect same-day delivery** — Uber's existing driver network in 70+ US metros is the lowest-incremental-cost asset to serve this demand vs. building dedicated fleets.
3. **Autonomous vehicle optionality is a free call option**: Uber's AV partnerships (Waymo, etc.) could structurally collapse last-mile cost if successful — not in consensus numbers.
4. **Freight brokerage (Uber Freight) benefits from trucking driver shortage** (60K–80K driver deficit): Tighter capacity = higher brokerage take rates; Uber's digital matching compresses search costs.
5. **P/E of ~18x is cheap for a platform business with 15–20% EBITDA growth visibility**: Market still values Uber as a ride-share company; logistics optionality is underpriced.

**Key Risks**
- Gig worker reclassification (AB5-style legislation) could structurally impair unit economics nationally.
- Uber Freight is cyclical and lost money for years; brokerage margins compress in soft freight markets.
- Core mobility remains the earnings engine — any ride-share disruption (AV competition, macro) overwhelms logistics upside in the near term.
