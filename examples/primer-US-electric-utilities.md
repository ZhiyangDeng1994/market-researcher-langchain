# US electric utilities — primer

## Overview
- US investor-owned utilities are on pace to spend approximately $207.9 billion on grid upgrades and expansions in 2025, the highest annual total ever recorded, up from $139.8 billion in 2020—a nearly 50% increase in five years. — *POWER Magazine, citing Edison Electric Institute Financial Review 2024, April 2025*
- EEI member utilities (investor-owned) are projected to make capital expenditures exceeding $1.1 trillion between 2025 and 2029, while Morningstar DBRS estimates total electricity infrastructure investment will reach $1.4 trillion from 2025 to 2030—double the amount invested in the prior 10 years. — *Utility Dive, 'US electric utilities entering investment super-cycle, says Morningstar DBRS,' October 27, 2025*
- Full-year 2025 utility capex is projected to rise 19% year-over-year to $215 billion, up from $173 billion in 2024, implying a 10.5% three-year CAGR—a sharp acceleration from the 7% CAGR of the prior decade. — *Gabelli.com, U.S. Utilities Research Report, 2025*
- Nearly 70% of North America's grid infrastructure is more than 25 years old (per DOE data), driving investment in system replacement, renewable integration, modernization, and weather resilience. Transmission rate base for 79 US electric utilities rose 7.6% to $185.14 billion in 2024, up from $172.02 billion in 2023. — *Gabelli.com, U.S. Utilities Research Report, 2025*
- In 2024, only 322 miles of new high-voltage (345 kV+) transmission lines were completed in the US—the third slowest year of such construction in the past 15 years—compared to nearly 4,000 miles built in 2013 alone. The DOE's 2024 National Transmission Planning Study calls for at least a doubling of regional transmission capacity and quadrupling of interregional capacity by 2050. — *Grid Strategies / Americans for a Clean Energy Grid, 'Fewer New Miles' Report, July 2025*
- FERC issued Order 1920 in 2024, the first federal mandate requiring transmission owners and planners to consider grid-enhancing technologies (GETs) such as dynamic line ratings and advanced power flow control devices in long-term transmission planning. By 2025, FERC had issued 69 compliance orders related to this rule. — *US Department of Energy, Idaho National Laboratory; FERC 2025 State of the Markets Report*
- As of end of 2024, approximately 10,300 projects representing 1,400 GW of generation and ~890 GW of storage were actively seeking grid interconnection in the US. A record 112 GW of solar and storage capacity combined withdrew from the queue in 2024, driven by political uncertainty, high interest rates, tariffs, and permitting challenges. — *Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, 'Queued Up: 2025 Edition,' December 2025*
- US total electricity consumption is projected to rise from approximately 4,110 billion kWh in 2024 to more than 4,260 billion kWh by 2026. The April 2025 five-year load growth forecast of 120 GW was more than five times higher than the 2023 forecast of just 23 GW. — *NZero, citing US Energy Information Administration Short-Term Energy Outlook, December 2025; Veckta, October 2025*
- US data centers are expected to consume approximately 176 TWh of electricity in 2025, with that figure projected to reach around 325 TWh by 2027—nearly doubling in two years—making data centers one of the fastest-growing sources of electricity demand in the country. — *Deloitte report, cited via US Department of Energy and IEA, December 2025*
- Smart electricity meter penetration in North America reached approximately 82% in 2024 and is expected to exceed 91% by 2030. North America recorded 18.5 million smart electricity meter shipments in 2024 alone, with the market now shifting from coverage expansion to advanced capability upgrades. — *Yahoo Finance / Berg Insight North America Smart Metering Industry Report 2025; Astute Analytica, 2025*
- The global smart grid market was valued at $41.44 billion in 2024 and is projected to grow to $155.16 billion by 2035, at a CAGR of 12.75%. North America dominated the market with the highest revenue share in 2025, driven by massive utility investments in grid modernization and renewables integration. — *Market Research Future, Smart Grid Market Report, updated April 2025; GlobeNewsWire / Astute Analytica, January 2026*
- The US has experienced 78 separate billion-dollar weather events over the past three years. Customer outage costs reached $121 billion in 2024, nearly double the seven-year average, while average outage durations have nearly doubled compared to a decade ago, spurring utilities to increase spending on advanced resilience technologies by 38% between 2023 and 2025—surpassing $8 billion. — *Deloitte, 'From Silos to Synergy: How Utilities Are Integrating AI and Geospatial Intelligence for Resilience,' April 2026 survey, reported via TD World*
- In 2025, approximately 63.3 GW of new utility-scale capacity was added to the US grid, with solar accounting for 52% (~33.3 GW), battery storage 29% (~18.3 GW), and wind 12% (~7.7 GW), while coal and nuclear net additions were zero. — *Baker Botts, '2025 US Energy Industry Commentary,' citing EIA data as of December 31, 2025*
- Projects reaching commercial operation in 2025 after entering the interconnection queue spent an average of eight years waiting, illustrating the severe bottleneck in the US interconnection process that delays clean energy deployment and drives up costs. — *Novoco / Rocky Mountain Institute analysis, citing LBNL and PJM data, October–December 2025*

## Shortlist: 5 Names

---

### 1. PPL Corporation (PPL) — *Regulated Utility Re-rating*

**Thesis Points**
- Clean post-UK-divestiture story: 100% US regulated T&D, Pennsylvania + Kentucky service territories with constructive rate cases; rate base growing ~10% annually through 2027 guided capex of ~$14.3B  `[UNSOURCED]`
- Pennsylvania is ground zero for hyperscaler load growth (mid-Atlantic data center corridor); PPL's territory borders PJM's most congested zones, giving it outsized transmission investment opportunity under FERC Order 1920
- EPS CAGR guidance of 6–8% through 2027 is achievable via formula rate mechanisms that compress regulatory lag—PPL earns returns faster than peers using traditional rate cases  `[UNSOURCED]`
- Trading at 12.6x EV/EBITDA vs. AEE at 13.7x despite similar balance sheet quality and superior load growth tailwinds; the discount is a re-rating catalyst as execution compounds  `[UNSOURCED]`
- Dividend yield ~3.2% provides return floor while capex cycle matures; payout ratio ~70% leaves room for dividend growth in line with earnings  `[UNSOURCED]`

**Key Risks**
- Pennsylvania PUC rate case outcomes are uncertain; any disallowance of grid modernization capex directly compresses allowed ROE (~9.5% currently)  `[UNSOURCED]`
- Rising debt load to fund capex could pressure BBB credit rating if interest coverage tightens in a prolonged high-rate environment
- Slower-than-expected data center load materialization in service territory would reduce transmission investment justification

### 2. Ameren Corporation (AEE) — *Transmission Rate Base Compounder*

**Thesis Points**
- FERC-regulated transmission is ~30% of AEE's rate base and grows under formula rates with near-zero regulatory lag; AEE is one of the top-5 transmission investors among EEI members by capex intensity relative to size  `[UNSOURCED]`
- Missouri PSC has approved a Grid Modernization Plan underpinning a $20B+ five-year capex program—explicit regulatory endorsement of spend that flows directly into rate base and earnings
- Midwest grid sits at the intersection of wind generation buildout and eastward power export demand; AEE's MISO footprint makes it a structural beneficiary of interregional transmission expansion called for in the DOE 2024 National Transmission Planning Study
- Conservative balance sheet (FFO/debt ~14–15%) and consistent 6–7% EPS CAGR guidance give it investment-grade credibility with income-oriented institutional holders—provides price stability while thesis plays out  `[UNSOURCED]`
- AEE has consistently earned its allowed ROE (9.8–10.0%) and has never had a major capex disallowance in Missouri—regulatory track record reduces execution risk vs. peers  `[UNSOURCED]`

**Key Risks**
- Premium valuation (13.7x EV/EBITDA, 20.3x P/E) leaves limited margin of safety; any earnings miss or rate case disappointment will compress the multiple sharply  `[UNSOURCED]`
- Missouri is a single-state concentration risk; a shift in PSC composition or political environment could reset the regulatory compact
- Interest rate sensitivity: AEE issues long-duration debt regularly; 50bps rate increase meaningfully reduces ROE spread and slows EPS growth

### 3. Edison International (EIX) — *Contrarian Wildfire Discount*

**Thesis Points**
- Trading at 8.6x EV/EBITDA and 8.3x P/E—the cheapest regulated utility in the peer set by a wide margin—entirely due to 2025 LA wildfire liability overhang, not underlying business deterioration  `[UNSOURCED]`
- Southern California is one of the highest load-growth geographies in the US: data centers, EV adoption, industrial electrification, and desalination projects create a multi-decade demand curve that underpins rate base expansion regardless of wildfire outcome
- California's CPUC has an established inverse condemnation framework and AB 1054 wildfire fund that provides partial structural liability protection; EIX has already demonstrated ability to securitize losses (Woolsey Fire) and recover through rates over time
- If wildfire liabilities are resolved at the lower end of analyst estimates (~$4–6B net of insurance and fund recovery vs. bear case $12B+), EPS normalizes to $4.50–5.00+, implying a stock price 40–60% above current levels at peer multiples  `[UNSOURCED]`
- Grid hardening capex (~$6B allocated 2024–2028 specifically for wildfire mitigation) is fully recoverable through rates under California's wildfire mitigation plan statute—capex risk is lower than perceived

**Key Risks**
- Litigation risk is genuinely binary: if courts assign EIX full liability for the January 2025 Eaton Fire (~$30B+ in insured losses), equity could be impaired severely
- California regulatory environment is the most politicized in the US; CPUC could disallow portions of wildfire spend or restructure allowed ROE downward under political pressure
- Execution risk on grid hardening is high—failures during 2025–2027 fire seasons before hardening is complete would compound liability exposure

### 4. Itron, Inc. (ITRI) — *Grid Intelligence Pure-Play*

**Thesis Points**
- Itron is the dominant provider of smart meters and distributed intelligence endpoints in North America; with penetration moving from 82% to 91%+ by 2030 and the market shifting from deployment to capability upgrades (AMI 2.0, grid-edge computing), ITRI captures replacement and upsell cycles simultaneously  `[UNSOURCED]`
- Outcomes software and managed services (Outcomes segment) now ~30% of revenue and growing faster than hardware; this mix shift drives margin expansion—Outcomes gross margins ~60%+ vs. ~35% for devices—and compresses cyclicality  `[UNSOURCED]`
- FERC Order 1920 explicitly requires utilities to evaluate Grid-Enhancing Technologies in long-term planning; ITRI's network solutions (dynamic line ratings integration, distributed automation) are direct beneficiaries of mandated planning workflows at 79+ transmission owners
- EV managed charging and distributed energy resource management (DERMS) are nascent revenue lines that are not yet in consensus estimates; the 176 TWh → 325 TWh data center demand surge also drives AMI network densification as utilities need real-time visibility
- Trading at 12.8x EV/EBITDA—in-line with regulated utilities despite 15%+ revenue growth and margin expansion story; the market is pricing ITRI as a commodity hardware vendor, not a grid intelligence platform  `[UNSOURCED]`

**Key Risks**
- Hardware revenue is lumpy and tied to utility capital budgets; any utility rate case freeze or financing stress reduces meter deployment orders with 6–12 month notice
- Tariff exposure: ITRI sources components from Asia; Section 301 tariffs or new semiconductor tariffs could compress hardware margins before price increases can be passed through
- Competition from Landis+Gyr, Honeywell, and Sensus (Xylem) is intensifying; customer concentration (top 10 utilities ~40%+ of revenue) creates renewal risk on large contracts  `[UNSOURCED]`

### 5. Eversource Energy (ES) — *Northeast Transmission Catalyst*

**Thesis Points**
- Eversource owns the largest transmission system in New England—a region with the most acute capacity shortage, highest wholesale power prices in the continental US, and near-zero new gas pipeline capacity—making ES the essential infrastructure solution for regional reliability
- Post-offshore wind exit (completed 2024), ES is now a clean regulated T&D story with no more development-stage earnings volatility; management has re-focused entirely on rate base growth with a $23.1B five-year capex plan (2024–2028), ~60% transmission-weighted  `[UNSOURCED]`
- Connecticut and Massachusetts are among the most constructive regulatory states for transmission investment: both have approved multi-year rate plans with formula rates that reduce regulatory lag and provide earnings visibility 3–5 years forward
- Load growth from Boston-area hyperscalers and EV mandates (MA ZEV rules) is among the highest in the Northeast; ISO-NE's 5-year forecast was revised sharply upward in 2024, directly supporting transmission expansion justifications in rate filings
- 11.8x EV/EBITDA is a discount to AEE despite a higher-quality regulatory jurisdiction and cleaner earnings profile post-offshore wind; re-rating to 13x+ is achievable on execution alone  `[UNSOURCED]`

**Key Risks**
- Balance sheet is stretched after offshore wind write-downs (~$1.6B total charges); FFO/debt of ~12% is below Moody's 13% threshold, and equity issuance dilution (~$1.5B guided 2025–2026) is a near-term headwind to EPS  `[UNSOURCED]`
- New England permitting timelines for new transmission lines are among the longest in the US (10–15 years siting-to-completion); capex plan execution risk is real even with regulatory support
- Rate case pushback from industrial and municipal customers facing some of the highest retail rates in the US could slow approved capex or compress allowed ROE below the current ~10.0–10.5% range  `[UNSOURCED]`
