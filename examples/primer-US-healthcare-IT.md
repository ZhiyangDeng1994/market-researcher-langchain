# US healthcare IT — primer

## Overview
- The US AI-in-healthcare market was valued at $14.92 billion in 2024 and is projected to reach $21.66 billion in 2025, growing to $110.61 billion by 2030 at a CAGR of 38.6%. — *Precedence Research / PRN Newswire, via DemandSage, 2025*
- 71% of non-federal acute-care US hospitals reported using predictive AI integrated into their EHR systems in 2024, up from 66% in 2023 — a significant year-over-year jump in institutional adoption. — *ONC/HealthIT.gov Hospital Trends Data Brief, cited in IntuitionLabs AI in Hospitals 2025 Adoption Trends report, October 2025*
- 66% of US physicians were using AI tools in practice by 2024, a 78% increase from 2023, according to an American Medical Association survey. — *American Medical Association (AMA) Digital Health Survey, cited in IntuitionLabs AI in Hospitals 2025, October 2025*
- 22% of healthcare organizations have implemented domain-specific AI tools as of 2025 — a 7x increase over 2024 and 10x over 2023 — with health systems leading at 27% adoption vs. only 9% across the broader economy. — *Menlo Ventures, 2025: The State of AI in Healthcare (survey of N=700), 2025*
- Healthcare-specific generative AI spending hit $1.4 billion in 2025, nearly tripling 2024 investment levels — making healthcare one of the fastest-growing verticals in enterprise AI, behind only horizontal chatbots and coding assistants. — *Menlo Ventures, 2025: The State of AI in Healthcare, 2025*
- Ambient AI scribing is healthcare AI's breakout category, generating $600 million in US revenue in 2025 (+2.4x year-over-year). Market leaders are Nuance DAX Copilot (33% share), Abridge (30%), and Ambience (13%). — *Menlo Ventures, 2025: The State of AI in Healthcare, 2025; corroborated by Zion Market Research Generative AI in Healthcare Market Report, 2025*
- Digital health venture funding reached $6.4 billion in H1 2025, slightly above the $6 billion raised in H1 2024, with AI startups leading momentum. The average deal size climbed to $26.1 million as later-stage rounds gained strength. — *VentionTeams, AI in Healthcare 2025 Statistics: Market Size, Adoption, Impact, 2025*
- Administrative AI attracted 60% of all healthcare AI investment in 2024, reflecting the acute burden of paperwork: 35% of healthcare professionals report spending more time on admin tasks than with patients, and clinicians spend nearly 28 hours per week on administrative duties. — *VentionTeams, AI in Healthcare 2025 Statistics: Market Size, Adoption, Impact, 2025; DialogHealth AI in Healthcare Statistics, 2025*
- Physician burnout fell to 41.9% in 2025, down from 43.2% in 2024 and 48.2% in 2023 (survey of ~19,000 physicians), with AI use addressing administrative burdens cited as a main driver of the decline. — *DialogHealth, 100+ Latest AI in Healthcare Statistics, 2025–2026*
- The FDA has approved a cumulative total of 1,016 AI/ML-enabled medical devices, with 989 of those approvals occurring in the past decade. Radiology accounts for 766 approvals and cardiovascular care for 103. — *Nature Digital Medicine (Park et al., June 2025), via CXOTech Magazine Healthcare AI: Regulatory Growth and Implementation Reality, 2025*
- In December 2024, the FDA issued final guidance on Predetermined Change Control Plans (PCCPs) for AI/ML systems, enabling post-market algorithm evolution while maintaining safety oversight — a key regulatory milestone for continuously learning AI tools. — *CXOTech Magazine, Healthcare AI: Regulatory Growth and Implementation Reality, citing FDA December 2024 final guidance, 2025*
- North America accounted for approximately 43% of global generative AI in healthcare revenue in 2025, underpinned by EHR adoption exceeding 96% of US hospitals; approximately 31.5% of US hospitals had implemented generative AI integrated with EHR systems by 2024. — *Zion Market Research, Generative AI in Healthcare Market Research Report, 2025; JAMA Network study, December 2025*
- Kaiser Permanente deployed Abridge's ambient documentation solution across 40 hospitals and over 600 medical offices — described as the largest generative AI rollout in healthcare history. Mayo Clinic is separately investing over $1 billion in AI across more than 200 projects. — *DialogHealth, 100+ Latest AI in Healthcare Statistics, 2025–2026*
- 57% of US physicians surveyed in 2024 cited automating administrative burden as the top opportunity for AI, while 84% said top attributes to advance adoption include EHR integration, data privacy assurances, and a designated feedback channel. — *DialogHealth, 100+ Latest AI in Healthcare Statistics, citing AMA 2024 survey data, 2025*
- AI adoption in US hospitals is highly uneven: states like New Mexico (0.00%), Mississippi (1.85%), and Idaho (1.96%) show near-zero hospital AI adoption, raising significant equity concerns about an urban/rural digital divide in healthcare quality. — *JAMIA (Journal of the American Medical Informatics Association), cited in DemandSage AI in Healthcare Statistics, 2025*

# US Healthcare IT — AI Adoption: Idea Shortlist

---

## 1. VEEV (Veeva Systems)
**EV: $24.3B | EV/EBITDA: 23.6x | Price: $193.67**

### Thesis Points
1. **Vault CRM + AI layer is a structural moat** — Veeva's migration from Salesforce to its own Vault CRM, now embedding AI across clinical data management and commercial ops, deepens switching costs in an already locked-in life sciences customer base
2. **Healthcare AI tailwinds amplify existing workflows** — with 96%+ US hospital EHR adoption as the backbone, Veeva's data network (>1,000 biopharma clients) positions it to monetize AI-assisted regulatory submissions and clinical trial automation
3. **Recurring revenue durability** — ~80% subscription mix with net retention >100% insulates against macro; AI upsell layers onto existing contracts rather than requiring greenfield budget
4. **Regulatory AI credentialing gap favors incumbents** — FDA's PCCP guidance (Dec 2024) creates compliance complexity that favors established platforms with audit trails over point solutions
5. **Vault Data Cloud as a long-term wedge** — proprietary clinical data assets are the feedstock for future AI products; competitors cannot replicate the dataset without the customer relationships

### Key Risks
- **Valuation leaves little room for error** at ~23.6x EBITDA vs. peers; any guidance miss re-rates sharply
- **Salesforce counter-attack** — incumbent CRM vendors rebuilding life-sciences verticals with AI (e.g., Einstein) could slow Vault CRM adoption
- **Concentration in biopharma spend** — biotech funding cycles directly impact Veeva's new logo growth; a prolonged capital-markets drought hits pipeline

---

## 2. DOCS (Doximity)
**EV: $3.3B | EV/EBITDA: 14.6x | Price: $22.26**

### Thesis Points
1. **Largest verified US physician network = irreplaceable distribution** — 80%+ of US physicians on platform; as 66% of physicians now use AI tools (AMA, 2024), Doximity is the natural distribution layer for AI-powered clinical workflows
2. **Ambient AI and clinical communication convergence** — Doximity's AI-drafting tools (already embedded in its messaging/EHR connectivity layer) sit directly in the $600M ambient scribing market without requiring a standalone product sale
3. **High-margin, asset-light model** — ~40%+ EBITDA margins; any AI monetization drops through at near-100% incremental margin given zero marginal distribution cost across existing user base
4. **Pharma marketing channel durability** — physician-targeted pharma ad spend is structurally non-discretionary and benefits from AI-personalized targeting, which Doximity is actively building
5. **Underappreciated re-acceleration potential** — stock de-rated heavily in 2023–24 on pharma ad softness; recovery in biotech/pharma DTC spend plus AI product launches represent a dual catalyst

### Key Risks
- **Revenue concentration** — top 20 pharma customers represent a large share of revenue; any consolidation or budget shift is high-impact
- **Ambient scribing competition** — Nuance (MSFT), Abridge, Ambience are better-capitalized or faster-moving in the core scribing category
- **Network commoditization** — LinkedIn Health and Epic's own communication tools could erode Doximity's unique physician access over time

---

## 3. TDOC (Teladoc Health)
**EV: $2.0B | EV/EBITDA: 43.0x | Price: $9.72**

### Thesis Points
1. **Deep value / self-help story** — stock is down ~95% from peak; EV now below 1x revenue, pricing in near-terminal outcomes that overstate structural deterioration; any execution improvement is asymmetric to the upside
2. **AI integration into chronic care management is a real differentiator** — BetterHelp + Livongo chronic care platform sit atop longitudinal patient data that can underpin AI-personalized care pathways; physician burnout reduction (41.9% in 2025) drives employer demand for virtual-first triage
3. **Administrative AI reduces TDOC's own cost structure** — 28 hrs/week of clinician admin burden is internal to Teladoc as much as external; AI scribing and care coordination tools can structurally improve unit economics
4. **Employer benefits channel is resilient** — large self-insured employers seeking to contain costs are accelerating virtual care adoption; TDOC's B2B channel insulates from consumer churn
5. **M&A optionality** — at current EV, TDOC is acquirable by a payer (CVS, UnitedHealth) or a tech platform (Amazon Health, Microsoft) seeking patient engagement infrastructure

### Key Risks
- **BetterHelp secular headwinds** — mental health telehealth faces pricing pressure, FTC scrutiny, and consumer fatigue; no clear path to profitability in this segment
- **Goodwill overhang** — $9.6B Livongo write-down history; balance sheet complexity and negative EPS (-$1.02) limit institutional sponsorship
- **Competition from vertically integrated payers** — UnitedHealth/Optum and CVS Health building captive virtual care solutions that disintermediate TDOC's employer channel

## 4. HIMS (Hims & Hers Health)
**EV: $9.0B | EV/EBITDA: ~107x | Price: $37.17**

### Thesis Points
1. **AI-personalized prescription workflow is a structural advantage** — Hims is building AI-driven intake, diagnosis support, and treatment personalization at consumer scale; 66% physician AI adoption (AMA) validates the clinical workflow, while Hims captures the direct-to-consumer channel above it
2. **Compounding pharmacy + GLP-1 exposure** — explosive GLP-1 demand (obesity, diabetes) provides near-term revenue rocket fuel; Hims' ability to compound semaglutide (while FDA shortage persists) is a rare regulatory arbitrage
3. **Subscription model drives LTV expansion** — average subscriber revenue growing as product breadth expands (MH, derm, weight, sexual health); AI cross-sell recommendations accelerate product attach rates
4. **Telehealth infrastructure as AI delivery vehicle** — Hims' licensed provider network + pharmacy fulfillment is a full-stack healthcare AI delivery system in a way pure-software peers are not
5. **Brand + consumer trust in underserved categories** — stigmatized conditions (ED, hair loss, mental health) where traditional care access is low; Hims removes friction in a way health systems cannot replicate quickly

### Key Risks
- **GLP-1 compounding cliff** — FDA has signaled enforcement against compounded semaglutide once shortage status ends; this could remove a large and fast-growing revenue stream abruptly
- **Regulatory/FTC headline risk** — DTC telehealth and online prescribing practices face increasing Congressional and regulatory scrutiny
- **Valuation is demanding** — 107x EBITDA embeds near-perfect execution; multiple compression on any miss would be severe given thin current profitability

---

## 5. OMCL (Omnicell)
**EV: $2.1B | EV/EBITDA: 28.4x | Price: $46.82**

### Thesis Points
1. **Pharmacy automation is a direct AI adoption beneficiary** — with 71% of acute-care hospitals already using predictive AI in EHRs, Omnicell's automated dispensing cabinets and Central Pharmacy services are the physical infrastructure layer that AI-driven medication management runs on top of
2. **XT Series + Intelligent Infrastructure upgrade cycle** — large installed base (~70% of US health systems) undergoing platform modernization; each refresh is an AI/analytics upsell opportunity (Advanced Services segment)
3. **SaaS transition inflects margins** — shift from hardware/capex to XR2 robotics-as-a-service and cloud-based pharmacy management (EnlivenHealth) should structurally improve EBITDA margins toward ~15–18% over 3 years from current ~10%
4. **Administrative AI tailwinds reduce pharmacy labor burden** — 35% of healthcare professionals cite admin overload; pharmacy workflow AI is a $2B+ addressable market where Omnicell has embedded relationships
5. **Capital discipline improving** — new management (2023–24) executing restructuring; FCF generation expected to inflect positive, reducing leverage and creating room for shareholder returns

### Key Risks
- **Hospital capex budget sensitivity** — large system purchases are deferrable; any macro-driven hospital budget tightening hits Omnicell's hardware revenue first
- **SaaS transition execution risk** — revenue recognition shift from upfront to ratable creates near-term headwinds; execution stumbles have burned investors before (2022–23 guidance cuts)
- **Competitive pressure from BD (Pyxis) and Swisslog** — Omnicell faces well-capitalized incumbents in dispensing; pricing power is limited in competitive RFPs
