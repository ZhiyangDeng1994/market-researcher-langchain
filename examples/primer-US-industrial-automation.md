# US industrial automation — primer

## Overview
- The global industrial automation market is projected to grow from $143.9 billion in 2024 to $212.4 billion by 2030, at a CAGR of 6.7%, with reshoring and near-shoring trends in the US acting as a significant structural tailwind. — *Valuates Reports, Global Industrial Automation Market Insights, Forecast to 2030 (2024)*
- US private manufacturing construction spending reached a record high of $240.1 billion (annualized) in August 2024 — a 214% increase over January 2021 levels — driven by the CHIPS Act, the Inflation Reduction Act, and corporate reshoring decisions. — *Manufacturing Lead Generation / FRED / U.S. Census Bureau, Manufacturing Construction Statistics (2025–2026)*
- The CHIPS Act and Inflation Reduction Act collectively triggered nearly $1 trillion in announced private manufacturing projects by late 2024, with subsidy eligibility tied to smart-manufacturing standards such as digital twins, OPC UA interoperability, and real-time energy dashboards — accelerating automation adoption by an estimated 18–24 months. — *Mordor Intelligence, North America Smart Manufacturing Market Size and Share Report (2025)*
- Reshoring and foreign direct investment brought 244,000 announced manufacturing jobs back to the US in 2024 — the third-highest annual total on record — with domestic US reshoring outpacing FDI by the largest margin ever recorded. The cumulative total since 2010 has surpassed 2 million jobs. — *Reshoring Initiative 2024 Annual Report, cited by KORE1 (2026) and Camoin Associates (2025)*
- 88% of reshored manufacturing jobs announced in 2024 were classified as high-tech or medium-high-tech, requiring robotics, machine vision, PLC/SCADA systems, and automation from day one — fundamentally changing the automation equipment demand profile of new facilities. — *KORE1, 'Reshoring in 2026: US Manufacturing Jobs Comeback' (2026), citing Reshoring Initiative data*
- Electrical equipment (EV batteries, solar components) and computer/electronics manufacturing (semiconductors) together drove two-thirds of all announced reshoring jobs in 2024, accounting for 31% and 35% of the total respectively — the two sectors most reliant on advanced factory automation. — *KORE1, 'Reshoring in 2026: US Manufacturing Jobs Comeback' (2026)*
- The US had 393,700 industrial robots in its operational stock as of 2024 (up 3% year-on-year), but China's stock of 2,027,200 units is roughly five times larger — highlighting a significant automation density gap that reshoring advocates argue must be closed to maintain competitiveness. — *International Federation of Robotics (IFR), World Robotics 2025 Report — Industrial Robots (September 2025)*
- US industrial robot installations fell 9% to 34,200 units in 2024, but North America overall is expected to see a tariff-driven shift of installations toward the United States in 2025, partly at the expense of Canada and Mexico. Global robot installations are forecast to grow 6% to 575,000 units in 2025, surpassing 700,000 by 2028. — *IFR, World Robotics 2025 Report — Executive Summary and Press Release (September 25, 2025)*
- US industrial robot installations rebounded sharply to 38,000 units in 2025, representing 11% year-on-year growth, driven by robust expansion in the food industry and other non-automotive sectors — a recovery consistent with new greenfield factory completions. — *International Federation of Robotics (IFR) / Business Wire, 'US Robot Installations Hit Double-Digit Growth' (2025 preliminary statistics)*
- The North America smart manufacturing market was valued at $62.21 billion in 2025 and is forecast to reach $91.08 billion by 2031 (CAGR 6.56%). The United States alone accounted for 71.53% of regional revenue in 2025, and federal reshoring incentives are credited with embedding digital-twin and edge-AI architectures into new greenfield plants. — *Mordor Intelligence, North America Smart Manufacturing Market Size and Share Report (2025)*
- US manufacturing is projected to need 3.8 million new workers between 2024 and 2033, but roughly 1.9 million of those roles may go unfilled due to skills and applicant gaps — a structural labor shortage that is a primary accelerant of automation investment in reshored facilities. — *Deloitte and The Manufacturing Institute, 'Taking Charge: Manufacturers Support Growth with Active Workforce Strategies' (2024 Talent Study), reported by Manufacturing Digital*
- 65% of US manufacturing companies cite recruiting and retaining workers as their top challenge (Deloitte survey), directly compelling plant operators — especially at newly reshored facilities — to substitute capital automation for scarce labor. — *Automation.com / Deloitte, 'What 2025 Taught Us About the Future of Manufacturing' (November 2025)*
- US reshoring investment escalated from $600 billion in Q1 2023 to $1.7 trillion by end of 2024 — a near-tripling in less than two years — creating the largest single pipeline of new factory-floor automation demand in decades. — *MCF Corporate Finance, Industrial Automation Industry Update Q3 2025 (2025)*
- Semiconductor projects from October 2024 to April 2025 represented only ~5% of US reshoring announcements by count but accounted for $102.6 billion — roughly two-thirds of all foreign capital invested — with mega-projects from TSMC, Samsung, and Intel embedding advanced automation as a contractual condition of CHIPS Act grants. — *Camoin Associates, 'Reshoring Reality: What's Fueling the Manufacturing Revival?' (2025)*
- 56% of manufacturers are currently piloting smart-factory systems and 20% are actively scaling them, while 95% plan to invest in AI or machine learning within five years — indicating that the automation build-out triggered by reshoring is broadening from hardware installation into software and AI-driven process control. — *Rockwell Automation, State of Smart Manufacturing Report 2025, cited by Develop LLC / Robotics Statistics for Manufacturers (2025)*

## Ideas
# US Industrial Automation — Reshoring-Driven Demand: Investment Shortlist

## Valuation Context
*Sector median EV/EBITDA: ~22x | Sector median P/E: ~25x*

---

## 1. Rockwell Automation (ROK) — **Highest Conviction**

**EV/EBITDA: 28x | P/E: 48x** *(premium to peers, but pure-play justification)*

### Thesis Points
1. **Pure-play leverage:** ROK is the only large-cap pure-play on US factory automation — ~100% of revenue tied to PLC/SCADA, MES, and intelligent devices; every reshored greenfield plant is a potential ROK customer with no revenue dilution from aerospace or HVAC segments.
2. **Software mix re-rating catalyst:** Plex and Fiix acquisitions push recurring ARR toward 25%+ of revenue; software gross margins (~75%) structurally above hardware (~45%), creating a durable re-rating story as the install base converts to subscription.
3. **CHIPS Act / IRA direct beneficiary:** Semiconductor and EV battery fabs — the two largest reshoring categories (66% of 2024 announced jobs) — require OPC UA-compliant, digital-twin-ready architectures where ROK's FactoryTalk suite is the incumbent standard embedded in CHIPS Act compliance frameworks.
4. **Labor substitution urgency:** With 1.9 million manufacturing roles projected unfillable, ROK's cobots, AGVs (via partnerships), and AI-driven process control directly address the #1 stated pain point of 65% of US manufacturers — making capex defense-of-operations, not discretionary.
5. **Cycle trough already in price:** ROK guided down through FY2024–25 on inventory digestion; robot installations rebounded 11% in 2025 — the earnings inflection aligns with greenfield completions moving from construction to fit-out phase.

### Key Risks
- **Valuation is unforgiving:** At 28x EV/EBITDA, any macro-driven capex pause (tariff uncertainty, credit tightening) compresses multiple before earnings recover.
- **Competitive displacement by Siemens/Fanuc:** Non-US automation giants are pricing aggressively into reshored fabs; ROK's US premium may erode in semiconductor mega-projects where Asian OEMs are specified by the fab operator.
- **Customer concentration:** Auto sector historically ~30%+ of revenue; EV transition capex pause is a near-term headwind.

---

## 2. Emerson Electric (EMR) — **High Conviction**

**EV/EBITDA: 15x | P/E: 32x** *(discount to ROK; post-restructuring multiple compression creates entry)*

### Thesis Points
1. **Post-AspenTech restructuring optionality:** The sale of Climate Technologies and consolidation around process/hybrid automation creates a cleaner industrial software + automation story; consensus has not yet fully re-rated the new EMR multiple toward software-hardware peers.
2. **Process automation dominance in reshoring's largest verticals:** EMR's DeltaV DCS and Ovation platforms are the standard in chemical, LNG, and semiconductor fabs — directly capturing the $102.6B semiconductor reshoring wave where process control is non-negotiable.
3. **AI/digital twin embedded in core product:** EMR's "Boundless Automation" architecture (edge-to-cloud, open process automation) aligns precisely with the smart-manufacturing standards (OPC UA, digital twins, real-time energy dashboards) required for IRA subsidy eligibility.
4. **Geographic mix shift is favorable:** EMR generates ~55% of revenue in the Americas; as tariffs penalize Asian-manufactured automation equipment, EMR's US-based engineering and service network becomes a competitive moat for reshored facilities requiring local support.
5. **M&A as a value unlock:** Balance sheet capacity post-divestitures supports bolt-on acquisitions in industrial AI/analytics; each deal is immediately accretive given EMR's integration track record and 15x entry multiple vs. software peers at 30x+.

### Key Risks
- **AspenTech minority stake overhang:** Complex ownership structure creates governance uncertainty and limits full consolidation of high-margin software earnings.
- **Execution risk on transformation:** Investors have been burned by prior EMR restructurings; the new portfolio thesis requires sustained execution over 3+ years.
- **Energy capex sensitivity:** Meaningful exposure to oil & gas process automation — a segment vulnerable to energy price volatility and ESG-driven capex constraints.

---

## 3. Rexnord/Regal Rexnord (RRX) — **Differentiated Bet**

**EV/EBITDA: 16x | P/E: 49x** *(EV/EBITDA at discount; P/E elevated on integration charges)*

### Thesis Points
1. **Under-the-radar reshoring pick:** RRX supplies motion control, couplings, bearings, and industrial powertrain components — the "boring infrastructure" inside every automated production line; demand is directly correlated with greenfield factory completions without the valuation premium of software-heavy peers.
2. **PMC acquisition creates a genuine automation platform:** The 2023 Regal Rexnord merger combined motion control hardware with variable-frequency drives and electric motors — now a one-stop power transmission supplier for the 38,000 new robot installations occurring annually in the US.
3. **Margin recovery story:** Integration synergies from the merger are still being realized; EBITDA margin expansion of 200–300 bps is achievable over 24 months, making EPS growth a function of execution rather than end-market heroics.
4. **Electrification tailwind within reshoring:** EV battery and solar component manufacturing — 31% of 2024 reshoring jobs — requires precision motion control and high-efficiency motors; RRX's efficiency-rated product line benefits from both IRA energy standards and DOE motor efficiency mandates.
5. **Lowest EV in the large-cap peer set:** At $18.7B EV vs. peers in the $55B–$130B range, RRX offers meaningful upside optionality if the market re-rates it toward the sector median post-integration.

### Key Risks
- **Integration execution is the central risk:** The Regal merger was large relative to the legacy balance sheet; any integration stumble (systems, culture, customer overlap) delays the margin story by 12–18 months.
- **Commodity input cost exposure:** Steel, copper, and rare earth magnets represent significant COGS; tariff-driven input inflation could compress gross margins faster than price increases can be pushed through.
- **Lower software content = lower re-rating ceiling:** Without a credible SaaS or recurring revenue narrative, RRX may remain range-bound at 15–18x EV/EBITDA even if execution is flawless.

---

## 4. AMETEK (AME) — **Quality Compounder**

**EV/EBITDA: 23x | P/E: 35x** *(slight premium to median; justified by acquisition engine)*

### Thesis Points
1. **Acquisition-compounding model is uniquely suited to the moment:** AME's playbook — acquire niche precision instrumentation businesses, extract 400–600 bps of margin, and cross-sell into existing channels — is perfectly timed as reshoring creates dozens of sub-scale automation component companies that become acquisition targets.
2. **Electronic instruments segment directly captures smart-manufacturing spend:** AME's test & measurement, process monitoring, and power quality instruments are required infrastructure for the digital-twin and real-time energy dashboard standards now embedded in IRA compliance — a non-discretionary spend category.
3. **Defense and aerospace diversification provides cycle protection:** ~30% of AME revenue is defense/aerospace electronics, providing earnings stability when industrial capex softens — a key differentiation vs. pure-play ROK.
4. **Consistent FCF conversion (>100% net income to FCF):** The capital-light nature of acquired niche businesses generates cash that funds the next acquisition without equity dilution — a self-reinforcing compounding machine with 15+ years of track record.
5. **Tariff pass-through power:** AME's products are highly engineered, low-volume, high-ASP instruments where customers have minimal substitution options — pricing power protects margins in a tariff-inflationary environment better than commodity automation hardware competitors.

### Key Risks
- **Acquisition pipeline quality degradation:** At $55B EV, AME needs larger deals to move the needle; larger targets carry more integration complexity and execution risk than the bolt-ons that built the franchise.
- **Valuation leaves little room for error:** 23x EV/EBITDA prices in continued execution; any organic growth miss (AME's organic growth has been choppy at 2–4%) re-rates the stock sharply.
- **Government budget exposure:** Defense electronics revenue is subject to continuing resolution risk and DoD budget prioritization shifts.

---

## 5. Ingersoll Rand (IR) — **Asymmetric Value Play**

**EV/EBITDA: 17x | P/E: 53x** *(EV/EBITDA at sector discount; P/E distorted by D&A from acquisition accounting)*

### Thesis Points
1. **Compressed multiple relative to peers is the primary thesis:** At 17x EV/EBITDA vs. sector median of ~22x, IR trades at a ~25% discount despite having a highly recurring aftermarket revenue stream (~40% of revenue), suggesting either a mispricing or a near-term catalyst event.
2. **Compressed air and fluid management are automation's hidden consumable:** Every automated production line requires compressed air systems (pneumatics), vacuum, and fluid handling — IR is the global #2 in industrial compression, meaning reshored factory fit-outs are a direct volume driver that is not yet reflected in current consensus estimates.
3. **ITS (Industrial Technologies & Services) segment re-rate potential:** IR has been systematically improving ITS margins post-Gardner Denver merger; segment EBITDA margins approaching 28–30% would justify a re-rating to 20x+, implying 20%+ upside on EV alone.
4. **Life sciences and semiconductor cleanroom air exposure:** IR's precision flow control and contamination-sensitive compressed air systems are specified into semiconductor fabs and pharmaceutical manufacturing — two of the highest-growth reshoring verticals — creating a defensible recurring maintenance stream.
5. **Balance sheet capacity for accretive M&A:** Net leverage at ~2x EBITDA post-recent deals leaves capacity for $3–5B of bolt-on acquisitions in adjacent precision flow control or industrial IoT — each deal could accelerate the multiple re-rating.

### Key Risks
- **P/E of 53x is optically alarming** and will deter value-sensitive institutional buyers until acquisition-related D&A amortization rolls off — potentially a 2–3 year wait for EPS normalization.
- **Competitive intensity in compression:** Atlas Copco and Kaeser are aggressive on pricing in large-project compression systems; IR's market share gains are not guaranteed in reshored mega-fabs where procurement is centralized.
- **Cyclical end-market exposure:** General industrial (non-semiconductor, non-life-sciences) compression demand is GDP-correlated; a manufacturing recession would compress volumes before the reshoring pipeline provides full offset.
