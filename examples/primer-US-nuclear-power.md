# US nuclear power — primer

## Overview
- NuScale Power remains the first and only SMR technology to have received design approval from the U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission. Its uprated 77 MWe US460 design received Standard Design Approval in May 2025, and ENTRA1 (its exclusive global commercialization partner) reached a nonbinding agreement with TVA to deploy up to 6 GW of NuScale capacity across TVA's seven-state service region. — *NuScale Power Press Release, 'NuScale Power Reports Fourth Quarter and Full Year 2025 Results,' 2026*
- The global SMR market was valued at approximately $4.8 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach $12.3 billion by 2034, reflecting a CAGR of 12.9%, driven by demand for low-carbon electricity, modular scalability, and increasing government investment. — *The Niche Research, 'Nuclear Small Modular Reactor (SMR) Market: Strategic Growth Insights 2025–2034,' July 2026*
- A separate market estimate pegged the SMR market at $159.4 million in 2024 on a narrower revenue basis, but projected explosive growth to $5.17 billion by 2035 at a 42.31% CAGR, with the 25–100 MW reactor segment leading adoption due to versatility and cost-effectiveness. — *ResearchAndMarkets.com / Globe Newswire, 'Small Modular Reactor Market Research Report 2025-2030,' August 2025*
- The U.S. Senate passed the ADVANCE Act (Accelerating Deployment of Versatile, Advanced Nuclear for Clean Energy) on June 18, 2024, mandating NRC to streamline licensing, reduce regulatory costs, and promote advanced reactor deployment including SMRs. The act also directs reduced fees for advanced nuclear applicants starting FY2026. — *Sidley Environmental, Health, and Safety Brief, 'Congress Passes ADVANCE Act to Facilitate U.S. Development of Advanced Nuclear Reactors,' June 27, 2024*
- President Trump signed four executive orders on May 23, 2025 to revitalize the U.S. nuclear industry, including EO 14300 directing the NRC to streamline licensing and revise regulations to balance safety with the benefits of emerging nuclear technology, building on the 2024 ADVANCE Act. — *U.S. NRC Office of Inspector General Report OIG-NRC-26-M-01, Oversight.gov, December 12, 2025*
- The NRC is developing a new risk-informed, technology-inclusive licensing pathway under 10 C.F.R. Part 53, designed to accommodate non-light-water reactor designs such as sodium-cooled fast reactors and high-temperature gas reactors, reducing reliance on prescriptive rules written for large conventional plants. — *Orrick Insights, 'NRC Issues New Licensing Pathway for Advanced Reactors,' April 2026*
- In March 2025, the DOE reissued a $900 million Gen III+ SMR solicitation to accelerate first-of-kind deployments. In December 2025, DOE selected TVA and Holtec International to each receive $400 million in cost-shared funding — supporting TVA's Clinch River BWRX-300 project and Holtec's planned SMR-300 units at the Palisades site. — *U.S. Department of Energy / World Nuclear Association, 'Nuclear Power in the USA,' updated 2025–2026; EIA Today in Energy, 2025*
- TerraPower's 345 MWe Natrium sodium-cooled fast reactor in Kemmerer, Wyoming — backed by up to $2 billion in DOE cost-share of a $4 billion total — received its NRC construction permit in March 2026, following site preparation begun in 2024 and a Wyoming state construction permit for non-nuclear portions in 2025. — *SMR Intel, 'SMR NRC Approval Tracker: Every Reactor in the Licensing Pipeline,' 2026; OECD NEA SMR Dashboard Third Edition, September 2025*
- Kairos Power's Hermes molten salt-cooled demonstration reactor received its NRC construction permit in December 2024 and began nuclear construction, targeting first power by 2027. Google contracted Kairos Power in October 2024 for up to seven SMRs totaling 500 MW of power for its data centers. — *OECD NEA SMR Dashboard Third Edition, September 2025; World Nuclear Industry Status Report 2025*
- In October 2024, Amazon led a $500 million investment round in X-energy and signed an MoU targeting deployment of more than 5 GW of Xe-100 SMR capacity by 2039. Amazon also contributed $334 million toward Energy Northwest's feasibility study for up to three 320 MW Xe-100 plants, securing first-offtake rights. — *World Nuclear Industry Status Report 2025; OECD NEA SMR Dashboard Third Edition, September 2025*
- Meta issued a Request for Proposals in December 2024 for up to 4 GW of new nuclear capacity, while Oracle stated it had secured permits for three SMRs to power its Stargate data centers. U.S. hyperscalers have emerged as the single largest new source of nuclear power demand, displacing utilities as primary off-takers. — *Reuters Events, 'Big Tech Shifts to All of the Above Strategy to Power AI,' December 11, 2025; tech-insider.org, 'X-energy IPO' analysis, 2025*
- In June 2025, the DOE launched the Energy Reactor Pilot Program, selecting vendors including Aalo Atomics, Oklo, Radiant Industries, Last Energy, and others to fast-track advanced reactor testing at sites outside national laboratories with a streamlined licensing pathway. — *U.S. Energy Information Administration, EIA Today in Energy, 2025*
- X-energy broke ground on a commercial TRISO nuclear fuel fabrication plant in the U.S. in 2025, addressing a critical supply chain bottleneck for high-temperature gas-cooled reactors and underpinning firm offtake agreements with Amazon and industrial partners. — *Transparency Market Research, 'Small Modular Nuclear Reactors Market Size and Forecast 2035,' 2025*
- The NRC's own Inspector General identified adapting legacy large-reactor regulations to SMRs as a top management challenge, warning that if licensing is not conducted in a timely manner, SMR deployment could be delayed and industry confidence in the NRC's readiness would erode. — *U.S. NRC Office of Inspector General Report OIG-NRC-26-M-01, Oversight.gov, December 12, 2025*
- As of early 2025, only three SMRs were commercially operating worldwide — China's HTR-PM, Russia's floating KLT-40S, and Japan's HTTR — underscoring that the U.S. SMR pipeline, while advancing rapidly, has yet to deliver a commercially operating unit. — *OECD NEA SMR Dashboard Third Edition, September 2025*

# SMR Commercialization — Investable Shortlist

---

## Screening Logic

Pre-revenue SMR pure-plays carry binary technology/licensing risk and trade on narrative. The actionable alpha sits in **picks-and-shovels enablers with current cash flows** plus **one high-conviction pure-play** where the risk/reward is asymmetric enough to justify the speculative weight.

---

## 1. BWX Technologies (BWXT) ★ Highest Conviction

**Thesis Points**
1. **Sole-source nuclear components manufacturer** — BWXT is the only U.S. supplier qualified to fabricate reactor pressure vessels and fuel assemblies at scale; every SMR that gets built in North America almost certainly flows through BWXT's supply chain
2. **Defense moat compounds the civil opportunity** — Naval nuclear propulsion contracts (~60% of revenue) provide recession-proof cash flow and keep the skilled workforce/manufacturing base fully utilized regardless of SMR commercialization timing
3. **BWRX-300 direct exposure** — TVA's Clinch River project (DOE $400M award) uses GE-Hitachi's BWRX-300, for which BWXT has a preferred supplier relationship on core components
4. **Earnings power already demonstrated** — $464M EBITDA, EPS $3.70; trades at ~38x EV/EBITDA, a premium but justifiable given the near-monopoly manufacturing position
5. **Capacity expansion underway** — Active investment in Lynchburg and Erwin facilities positions BWXT to capture the first major SMR build wave without the greenfield capital risk

**Key Risks**
- SMR construction delays push the civil revenue inflection out 3–5 years, compressing the growth premium in the multiple
- DoD budget cuts could pressure naval program revenues (low probability but high impact)
- Single-customer concentration risk if a key program is restructured

---

## 2. Constellation Energy (CEG)

**Thesis Points**
1. **Largest U.S. nuclear fleet operator** — 21 reactors, ~10% of U.S. electricity; uniquely positioned to benefit from power price re-rating as AI/data center demand absorbs baseload capacity
2. **Nuclear PTC windfall** — Inflation Reduction Act's $15/MWh production tax credit applies to existing fleet, adding ~$700M–$1B/year in after-tax cash flow at current prices
3. **SMR partnership optionality** — CEG has the balance sheet, NRC relationships, and site permits to be a first-mover SMR operator/developer without the pre-revenue technology risk
4. **Microsoft Three Mile Island restart** demonstrates hyperscaler willingness to pay premium pricing for 24/7 carbon-free power; CEG is the natural counterparty for similar deals
5. **$7.96B EBITDA, $258 stock** — EV/EBITDA ~14.5x, still a discount to gas-peaker peers (VST at ~11x but with far more commodity exposure) given the regulatory certainty of the nuclear PTC

**Key Risks**
- Policy reversal on nuclear PTC under future administration
- Electricity price normalization if gas prices collapse
- Regulatory/safety incident at any plant (reputational spillover)

---

## 3. Centrus Energy (LEU)

**Thesis Points**
1. **Only U.S.-licensed HALEU enricher** — High-Assay Low-Enriched Uranium is the fuel required by most advanced SMR designs (TerraPower Natrium, Xe-100, Kairos); Centrus holds a monopoly on domestic HALEU production capability
2. **Russian import ban creates structural tailwind** — HALEU sourced from Russia is now prohibited under the 2024 Prohibiting Russian Uranium Imports Act; Centrus is the only near-term domestic alternative
3. **DOE cost-share contracts de-risk capex** — DOE has funded Centrus's Piketon, Ohio HALEU cascade; additional contract awards likely as SMR pipeline firms up
4. **Existing LEU business generates cash** — $34M EBITDA provides a floor; the HALEU option is essentially free at current EV of $2.4B
5. **Supply constraint could be the binding SMR bottleneck** — If fuel supply, not licensing, becomes the gating factor, Centrus captures pricing power disproportionate to its current size

**Key Risks**
- HALEU demand timeline depends entirely on SMR construction starting — any 2–3 year slip is painful
- DOE could elect to fund competing enrichment capacity (urenco, centrifuge alternatives)
- Small float ($156/share, ~$800M market cap) = high volatility and illiquidity risk

---

## 4. GE Vernova (GEV)

**Thesis Points**
1. **BWRX-300 is the leading near-term SMR** — GEV's 300 MWe boiling water design has the furthest advanced licensing globally (Canada, U.S., Poland), DOE backing at Clinch River, and Ontario Power Generation as launch customer
2. **Grid infrastructure supercycle independent of SMR** — GEV's gas turbines, wind, and grid solutions businesses are already generating $3.4B EBITDA and growing; SMR is a free call option on an already-investable platform
3. **Execution credibility post-GE spin** — CEO Scott Strazik has demonstrated operational discipline; GEV is not a story stock — it has backlog, margins, and FCF
4. **Hyperscaler demand pulls forward the BWRX timeline** — If one hyperscaler signs an offtake for BWRX-300 capacity, GEV's order book re-rating could be substantial
5. **Manufacturing scale advantage** — GEV's global turbine and heavy manufacturing infrastructure gives it a cost and delivery credibility edge over startup SMR developers

**Key Risks**
- At $278B EV, SMR optionality is a small percentage of total value — upside attribution is limited vs. pure-plays
- Gas turbine super-cycle could cool if power demand projections disappoint
- BWRX-300 faces competition from BWXT-supplied designs and international vendors (Rolls-Royce SMR)

---

## 5. Oklo (OKLO) — Speculative Weight Only

**Thesis Points**
1. **Aurora fast reactor targets 15 MW microreactor niche** — Oklo's compact fission design addresses remote industrial, defense, and data center co-location markets not served by 300 MW+ SMRs; limited direct competition
2. **Sam Altman as Executive Chairman** — OpenAI CEO's involvement is a credible signal of hyperscaler demand; Oklo has a letter of intent pipeline reportedly exceeding 2 GW
3. **DOE Energy Reactor Pilot Program selection** validates technology readiness and provides a streamlined licensing pathway
4. **First-mover in used nuclear fuel recycling** — Oklo's longer-term business model includes actinide recycling, reducing fuel costs and waste; differentiated IP if it works
5. **$5.7B EV prices in significant success** — The market is already pricing a favorable outcome, but the asymmetry exists if Oklo secures a signed commercial contract and construction permit ahead of peers

**Key Risks**
- NRC rejected Oklo's first license application in 2022; resubmission is in process but regulatory risk is real and demonstrated
- $5.7B EV on -$172M EBITDA requires flawless execution over 5–7 years with zero revenue today
- Technology risk: fast spectrum reactors have a history of commercial disappointment (EBR-II, Monju, Superphénix)
- Any Altman distraction/departure would be a significant sentiment shock
