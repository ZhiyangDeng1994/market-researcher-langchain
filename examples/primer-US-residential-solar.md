# US residential solar — primer

## Overview
- The US residential solar market was valued at USD 13.84 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach USD 41.38 billion by 2034, growing at a CAGR of 12.94%, driven by rising electricity costs, declining technology costs, and (historically) supportive government incentives. — *MarketDataForecast, U.S. Residential Solar Market Size and Analysis, 2034 (2025)*
- The US residential solar segment installed 4,647 MWdc of capacity in full-year 2025, declining roughly 2% compared to 2024, as high interest rates, policy uncertainty, and module shortages constrained growth across 27 state markets. — *SEIA Solar Market Insight Report 2025 Year in Review (SEIA.org, 2025–2026)*
- The 'One Big Beautiful Bill' (H.R. 1), signed into law on July 4, 2025, eliminated the Section 25D residential solar investment tax credit (ITC) effective December 31, 2025 — a cliff-edge drop from 30% to 0% with no phase-down period, the most abrupt federal solar policy shift in nearly two decades. — *Infinity Solar / SolarInsure, 'Solar Investment Tax Credit Changes: What's Happening to the ITC?' (2025)*
- SEIA now projects residential solar installations will fall 19% year-over-year in 2026 following Section 25D expiration, with Wood Mackenzie predicting volumes will drop to their lowest level since the COVID-19 pandemic year of 2020 and not recover until the end of the decade. — *SEIA Solar Market Insight Report 2025 Year in Review; Daily Reporter / Reuters, 'U.S. Solar Industry Faces Job Cuts After Tax Credit Expires' (February 5, 2026)*
- Major installer disruptions have already materialized post-ITC: Enphase cut 160 jobs, Freedom Forever laid off ~20% of its workforce and exited 10 state markets, and Purelight Power filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy affecting ~200 workers, all cited directly as consequences of the credit's expiration. — *Daily Reporter / Reuters (via USA Today Network), 'As Subsidy Expires, Solar Industry Sees Job Cuts, Restructuring' (February 5, 2026)*
- Third-party ownership (TPO) structures — leases and PPAs — retain ITC eligibility through 2027 under the new law, providing a partial cushion; SEIA expects TPO eligibility and bonus adders to partially offset the 2026 contraction and contribute to recovery beginning in 2027. — *SEIA Solar Market Insight Report Q4 2025 (SEIA.org, 2025); Earthlight Tech, 'Federal Solar Tax Credit Changes in 2025' (2025)*
- California's NEM 3.0 (Net Billing Tariff), effective April 15, 2023, cut export compensation rates by roughly 75% — from ~$0.30/kWh to ~$0.05–$0.08/kWh — causing California residential solar permits to collapse 32% in 2024 (to 121,350 permits) after peaking at 187,511 in 2022. — *Shovels.ai, 'How Changing Energy Policies Are Transforming California Homes' (2025); Solar.com NEM 3.0 explainer (updated December 2025)*
- California's CPUC 2025 SB 695 Report found that under the legacy NEM 2.0 tariff, residential solar customers were compensated at ~$0.45/kWh for electricity exported to the grid that the utility's own 2024 Avoided Cost Calculator valued at only ~$0.05/kWh — a cost-shift dynamic that drove the NEM 3.0 reform and sets a template for other states. — *California Public Utilities Commission, 2025 Senate Bill 695 Report (CPUC, September 2025)*
- As of October 2025, at least 10 states had implemented new policies in direct response to declining federal solar support, and regulators in Nevada, Virginia, and West Virginia all reached decisions on net metering successor tariff proposals during 2025. — *National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR), Fall 2025 Solar Industry Update (docs.nlr.gov, 2025)*
- Nevada regulators approved changes in September 2025 requiring new solar customers in Northern Nevada (systems installed after October 1, 2025) to have credits calculated every 15 minutes rather than monthly — a more granular netting cycle that reduces the bill-offset value of solar-only systems and accelerates the push toward solar-plus-storage. — *Solar Permit Solutions, 'NV Energy Net Metering 2025: Nevada Solar Rates, Rebates & ROI Guide' (2025)*
- Illinois eliminated delivery credits for new solar owners starting January 1, 2025; new ComEd, Ameren, and MidAmerican customers now receive only supply credits for excess generation, significantly reducing the financial return of standalone solar systems in the state. — *NEDES.us, 'How Net Metering Policy Changes Are Affecting USA Homeowners' (2025)*
- Solar loan interest rates tripled from roughly 1.99% APR in late 2022 to 7.49%–8.5% by mid-2024 through 2025, adding 2–4 years to typical payback periods; high financing costs were cited alongside policy uncertainty as the two primary headwinds suppressing Q2 2025 residential installations, which fell 9% year-over-year. — *IntegrateSun, 'Is Solar Still Worth It with High Interest Rates? A 2025 Financial Analysis' (2025); SEIA Solar Market Insight Report Q3 2025 (SEIA.org, 2025)*
- On the trade-policy front, Chinese solar polysilicon, wafers, and cells now face 60% US tariffs under expanded Section 301 rules, steel and aluminum (used in racking and frames) face 50% tariffs, and a new 25% tariff on Indian solar imports took effect August 2025 — with AD/CVD investigations against India, Indonesia, and Laos advancing in parallel, threatening to further raise residential module costs. — *Aurora Solar, 'How the Latest Tariffs Could Impact Solar Energy in 2025' (2025); Sunhub, 'U.S. Solar Policy Shifts September 2025 Explained' (2025); PV Magazine USA, 'A Tightened U.S. Solar Trade Environment' (September 19, 2025)*
- A McKinsey analysis forecasts that US installed solar capacity could be 9% lower by 2035 under the highest-tariff scenario compared to the late-2024 status quo, reflecting the compounding drag of trade barriers on long-run deployment even as domestic manufacturing scales up. — *PV Magazine USA, 'A Tightened U.S. Solar Trade Environment' (September 19, 2025)*
- Despite policy headwinds, battery storage attachment rates rose from ~12% of new residential solar installations in 2020 to an estimated 35–40% in 2025–2026, driven largely by net metering erosion and expanding time-of-use rate structures — a structural shift that reframes residential solar economics around self-consumption rather than grid export. — *IndexBox, 'Residential Solar Energy Storage Market in the United States' (2025)*

## Ideas
# US Residential Solar — Policy & Rate Risk: Shortlist

---

## Macro Context in 30 Seconds

The sector faces a **simultaneous triple shock**: (1) 25D ITC eliminated cliff-edge end-2025, (2) net metering erosion state-by-state, (3) module/racking tariffs compounding cost. SEIA projects −19% residential installs in 2026 with no recovery until decade-end. TPO structures (leases/PPAs) retain ITC through 2027 — the single biggest structural dividing line in the comp set.

---

## The Shortlist

### 1. **ENPH — Enphase Energy** *(Short Bias)*
**Thesis Points:**
- Pure-play microinverter exposure to US residential; ~50% of revenue historically from US resi segment — exactly the market contracting 19% in 2026  `[UNSOURCED]`
- Already cut 160 jobs post-25D expiration; guidance likely embeds optimistic volume recovery assumptions not consistent with Wood Mac's "below 2020 levels" forecast
- EV/EBITDA ~30.6× on already-depressed EBITDA of $179mm — multiple pricing in recovery that may not arrive until 2028–2029
- No meaningful TPO revenue benefit; microinverters sell into installer channel which is restructuring (Freedom Forever −20% workforce, Purelight bankrupt)  `[UNSOURCED]`
- Battery storage attach rate tailwind (35–40%) partially offsets but requires ENPH IQ Battery to compete against cheaper alternatives (Tesla, Franklin)  `[UNSOURCED]`

**Key Risks to Short:** Storage growth accelerates faster than expected; European resi holds up; interest rate cuts revive US demand sharply

---

### 2. **RUN — Sunrun** *(Long Bias)*
**Thesis Points:**
- Largest US residential TPO provider — leases and PPAs retain ITC eligibility **through 2027**, the single most important policy carve-out in OBBB
- $18.9bn EV reflects existing contracted cash flow book (20–25 year agreements) not just new installation volume; book value of contracted future cash flows provides floor  `[UNSOURCED]`
- NEM 3.0 and successor tariffs structurally accelerate TPO adoption vs. ownership — customers can't monetize 25D themselves but can benefit indirectly via lower PPA rates funded by RUN capturing the credit
- Storage attach rates rising to 35–40% directionally favor RUN's grid services / VPP revenue model (NV Energy, PG&E partnerships)  `[UNSOURCED]`
- EPS of $2.13 suggests profitability already; EV/EBITDA ~27× reasonable if TPO volumes offset ownership collapse

**Key Risks to Long:** Warehouse/ABS financing costs remain elevated (rates sticky); 2027 TPO ITC cliff creates same binary risk one year out; customer credit quality deteriorates in recession

---

### 3. **FSLR — First Solar** *(Long Bias)*
**Thesis Points:**
- Cadmium telluride thin-film manufacturer with **no Chinese supply chain exposure** — structurally advantaged as 60% tariffs hit Chinese polysilicon/cells and AD/CVD investigations advance against India, Indonesia, Laos
- Domestically manufactured modules qualify for IRA Section 45X Advanced Manufacturing Production Credit (~$0.17/W), which is **not eliminated** by OBBB — unlike 25D, 45X is a manufacturer-side credit
- Revenue overwhelmingly utility-scale / commercial, not residential — limited direct exposure to 25D cliff; EV/EBITDA ~9.8× on $2.27bn EBITDA is the most defensible multiple in the comp set  `[UNSOURCED]`
- Order book of ~80+ GW (as of prior disclosures) provides multi-year revenue visibility at a time when competitors face demand uncertainty
- McKinsey's −9% installed capacity scenario by 2035 paradoxically benefits FSLR if tariff walls keep out foreign modules and domestic supply is constrained  `[UNSOURCED]`

**Key Risks to Long:** Utility-scale ITC/PTC also faces political risk in subsequent legislation; CdTe efficiency gap vs. silicon narrows; demand destruction in utility segment from power purchase agreement repricing

---

### 4. **SEDG — SolarEdge Technologies** *(Short Bias)*
**Thesis Points:**
- DC-optimized inverter business with heavy US and European residential exposure; already generating **−$170mm EBITDA** on $3.18bn EV — paying a premium for losses
- European resi also deteriorating (Germany, Netherlands net metering changes); no geographic safe harbor
- Inventory overhang and channel destocking have persisted 6+ quarters; 25D elimination removes the demand catalyst needed to clear excess
- EPS of −$6.03 with no credible path to profitability in 2026 given volume trajectory; balance sheet stress risk if credit facilities tighten
- Unlike ENPH, SEDG has less battery storage revenue diversification to offset inverter weakness

**Key Risks to Short:** European policy reversal or utility-scale pivot gains traction; M&A takeout at distressed valuation; short interest already elevated creating squeeze risk

---

### 5. **ARRY — Array Technologies** *(Selective Long / Monitor)*
**Thesis Points:**
- Single-axis tracker manufacturer — **utility-scale** not residential; largely insulated from 25D and net metering dynamics that are crushing the resi comp set
- EV/EBITDA ~16.2× on $124.8mm EBITDA; tariff exposure on steel/aluminum (50% tariffs) is real but Array has historically passed through material costs via contract escalators  `[UNSOURCED]`
- Utility-scale solar pipeline remains robust as data center / AI power demand drives commercial offtake — independent of residential policy cycle
- Negative EPS (−$0.85) reflects D&A and interest burden, not cash burn; free cash flow profile materially better than GAAP suggests
- Position as infrastructure equipment supplier (not installer) means no direct credit/financing risk from high consumer loan rates

**Key Risks:** Steel/aluminum tariff pass-through fails in competitive bid environment; utility-scale ITC at risk in future legislative sessions; project financing tightens for developers
