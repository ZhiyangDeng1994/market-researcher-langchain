# global LNG shipping — primer

## Overview
- Global LNG trade rose 6.3% to 436.98 million metric tons in 2025, the fastest rate of growth since 2022. The U.S. remained the world's largest LNG exporter at 110.74 million tons, followed by Qatar (81.51 Mt) and Australia (80.32 Mt). — *Reuters / International Gas Union (IGU) World LNG Report, July 2026*
- The global LNG carrier orderbook stood at approximately 51.3% of the active fleet by gross tonnage as of early 2025 — the largest proportional orderbook of any shipping segment, with 313 vessels on order versus 683 active units at end-2024. — *UNCTAD Review of Maritime Transport 2025*
- Planned global liquefaction capacity additions of over 300 bcm/year are set to come online between 2025 and 2030, on top of today's ~670 bcm/year. However, the 234 newbuild LNG carriers ordered to serve this capacity could support only ~78 million t/year of supply if routed entirely to Asia — far below what new production will require. — *bne IntelliNews / Argus Media, December 18, 2025*
- South Korean shipyards (HD Hyundai, Samsung HI, Hanwha Ocean) hold 66–75% of the global LNG carrier orderbook but are operating near maximum capacity. New orders placed in 2025 face delivery slots only in late 2028 or 2029, creating 3.5–4 year construction lead times. — *ShipFinex / Clarksons Research, 2025; iMarine News, 2025*
- LNG carrier newbuild prices hit approximately $250 million per vessel on average, partly driven by steel prices that peaked at $1,050/ton in 2023, raising construction costs by 20% versus 2022. Prices are forecast to exceed $260 million within 6–12 months as slot competition intensifies. — *MarketGrowthReports LNG Carrier Market Analysis, 2025; iMarine News, 2025*
- In 2025, 14 steam turbine LNG carriers were sold for demolition — nearly triple the annual average between 2020 and 2024 — thinning the active fleet just as new liquefaction supply is ramping up. Nine LNG carriers had already been scrapped before year-end 2025, surpassing all of 2024's total of eight. — *bne IntelliNews / Argus Media, December 2025; LinkedIn / Shyam Paliwal, 2025*
- Nearly 60 LNG carriers sat idle by late July 2025, while charter rates hit historic lows: two-stroke vessels in the Atlantic Basin averaged just $30,000/day and steam turbine vessels earned below $10,000/day — barely covering operational costs — due to a fleet oversupply relative to near-term cargo volumes. — *Wood Mackenzie, 'The Great LNG Shipping Reset,' 2025*
- For the ~20% of LNG carriers trading on the spot/short-term charter market, a one-year time charter rate for a DFDE vessel hit a low of $9,000/day in early 2025, while fleet capacity grew 17% across 2024–25 against LNG volume growth of only 7% over the same period. — *Clarksons Research, 'LNG Shipping: Short-Term Headwinds, Long-Term Growth,' July 18, 2025*
- The Panama Canal suffered LNG transit declines of up to 73% during severe El Niño drought conditions in 2023–2024. Vessel weight restrictions and reduced daily transits (from 38 to as few as 24 ships/day) forced LNG operators to reroute around Africa's Cape of Good Hope, a shift that has not fully reversed even after water levels recovered. — *CNBC, 'Inside Panama Canal Mega-Project Plan to Survive Severe Drought,' September 13, 2025*
- Red Sea and Bab el-Mandeb transits in 2025 fell to around 35–40% of 2023 baseline volumes due to persistent Houthi attacks on commercial vessels. Major LNG carriers avoided the Suez Canal route, adding 10–14 days per Asia-Europe voyage via the Cape of Good Hope. Suez Canal traffic in early 2026 remained roughly 60% below pre-crisis levels. — *ISDO / Maritime Geopolitics Analysis, February 2026; DocShipper Red Sea Crisis Update, December 2025*
- Global LNG liquefaction capacity currently stands at 745 bcm and is projected to grow by a further 200 bcm by 2030. However, 85–95% of a project's production is typically pre-sold under long-term contracts, leaving only 5–15% available for spot trading and limiting the market's flexibility to respond to price signals. — *ACER 2026 LNG Monitoring Report*
- Over 70% of new liquefaction capacity additions to 2030 will come from the United States and Qatar alone, further concentrating supply. In 2025, more than 90 bcm/year of LNG liquefaction capacity reached final investment decision — the second-highest annual amount on record after 2019. — *IEA Gas 2025 report; IEA News, 2025*
- Multiple major LNG export projects suffered construction and commissioning delays: Plaquemines LNG Phase 1 (13.33 MTPA), Golden Pass LNG Trains 1 and 2 (12.06 MTPA combined), and Corpus Christi Trains 4–10 (11.48 MTPA) were all pushed back from their 2024 start-up schedules to 2025 or later. — *BRS Group Annual Review 2025*
- Europe's LNG imports surged 26.1 million tons to 126.2 million tons in 2025, as the continent replaced dwindling Russian pipeline gas. This created structural competition with Asian buyers for spot cargoes, tightening the global supply chain and leaving Southeast Asian emerging economies exposed to elevated price volatility. — *Reuters / IGU World LNG Report, July 2026; IEA Gas 2025*

## Ideas
# LNG Shipping Supply Chain Bottleneck — Investment Shortlist

---

## Screening Rationale

The structural thesis hinges on **three converging forces**: (1) a ~300 bcm/yr liquefaction wave arriving 2025–2030, (2) a carrier fleet that is structurally undersupplied relative to long-haul demand once route dislocations (Red Sea, Panama) are normalized, and (3) 3.5–4 year newbuild lead times that make the supply response slow. Near-term spot rates are depressed (the "Great Reset"), creating an entry window before the tightening arrives. Names should have **long-term contract backlog** to survive the trough, balance sheet capacity to weather it, and leverage to the eventual rate recovery.

## Shortlist: 5 Names

---

### 1. **FLNG — Flex LNG Ltd.**
*Pure-play modern LNG carrier operator; 13 two-stroke TFDE/X-DF vessels, all under medium-to-long-term charters*

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| EV | $3,106mm |
| EV/EBITDA | 13.7× |
| Price | $30.95 |
| EPS | $1.40 |

**Thesis Points**
1. **Fleet quality hedge**: 100% two-stroke/TFDE fleet — zero steam turbine exposure as scrapping of older vessels thins the fleet. Positions FLNG as the preferred counterparty when charterers upgrade.
2. **Contract backlog insulation**: Multi-year fixed-rate charters provide cash flow visibility through the 2025–2026 spot-rate trough; FLNG does not need to roll vessels into a $9–30k/day spot market.
3. **Rate re-rating optionality**: As 2028–2029 newbuild deliveries absorb new liquefaction volumes, existing modern vessels will re-contract at structurally higher rates — FLNG's next charter roll is a call option on tightening.
4. **Dividend sustainability**: Covered dividend yield at current contract rates offers income while the thesis matures; modest leverage (~50% LTV) limits distress risk.
5. **Consolidation target**: Sub-$3.5bn EV makes FLNG digestible for a major energy company or larger shipping group seeking contracted fleet exposure.

**Key Risks**
- Re-contracting risk if multi-year charters roll into a still-oversupplied market (2026–2027 window).
- Counter-party credit risk on long-term charterers if LNG demand disappoints in Europe post-pipeline normalization.
- Norwegian/Bermuda holding structure creates withholding tax complexity for U.S. retail investors.

### 2. **GLNG — Golar LNG Ltd.**
*Integrated LNG infrastructure play: FLNG vessels (floating liquefaction), shipping, and Power/downstream exposure*

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| EV | $7,079mm |
| EV/EBITDA | 26.8× |
| Price | $50.70 |
| EPS | $1.26 |

**Thesis Points**
1. **FLNG infrastructure scarcity premium**: Golar's Hilli Episeyo and Gimi FLNG assets sit at the **chokepoint of the supply chain** — they convert stranded gas to LNG at the wellhead, bypassing land-based liquefaction congestion entirely. Hilli's tolling fee structure provides near-zero volume risk.
2. **Gimi / BP contract lock-in**: Gimi commenced operations on the Greater Tortue Ahmeyim project in 2025 under a 20-year contract with BP, adding ~$3bn in contracted backlog essentially not yet in consensus EBITDA estimates.
3. **Macro-independent cash flows**: Tolling model means Golar's FLNG revenues are indifferent to spot LNG prices — a differentiated profile versus pure shipping names levered to rate cycles.
4. **Pipeline of FLNG conversions**: With shipyard slots for newbuild LNG carriers 3.5–4 years out, FLNG conversions (which Golar pioneered) become the **fastest path** to new liquefaction capacity — Golar is uniquely positioned to win conversion mandates.
5. **NAV discount**: Sum-of-parts on Hilli, Gimi, Macaw (next conversion), and residual shipping stake suggests meaningful discount to intrinsic value at current price.

**Key Risks**
- EV/EBITDA of 26.8× prices in significant execution on Macaw and future conversion pipeline — delays compress the multiple rapidly.
- Gimi project ramp risk: offshore FLNG is operationally complex; any unplanned downtime directly hits contracted revenue.
- Corporate complexity (partial ownership of CoolCo, legacy shipping assets) makes earnings visibility lower than peers.

### 3. **KNOP — KNOT Offshore Partners LP**
*Shuttle tanker MLP — tangential but structurally relevant as a high-yield contracted infrastructure proxy*

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| EV | $1,291mm |
| EV/EBITDA | 6.4× |
| Price | $10.70 |
| EPS | $0.34 |

**Thesis Points**
1. **Deep value on contracted cash flows**: At 6.4× EV/EBITDA, KNOP trades at a steep discount to LNG carrier peers; shuttle tankers serving offshore oil fields carry similarly long-term fixed contracts with investment-grade counterparties (Equinor, Shell, Repsol).
2. **Indirect LNG bottleneck beneficiary**: As European energy security drives North Sea and Brazilian offshore production higher (to backstop LNG import demand), shuttle tanker utilization rises — KNOT is the dominant player in this niche.
3. **MLP yield compression trade**: If interest rates normalize and MLP structures regain yield-seeking capital, KNOP's distribution coverage and asset quality support meaningful unit price appreciation from depressed levels.
4. **Fleet renewal optionality**: KNOT AS (the sponsor) owns newer vessels that could be dropped down into the partnership, extending backlog and supporting distributions.
5. **Scarcity of specialized tonnage**: Shuttle tankers are even more niche than LNG carriers — fewer than 90 purpose-built vessels exist globally, limiting competitive entry.

**Key Risks**
- MLP structure complexity and K-1 tax reporting deters institutional investors, keeping the valuation discount structural rather than temporary.
- Sponsor KNOT AS is privately held by a consortium; dropdown pipeline pace is sponsor-controlled and opaque.
- Oil price downturn could reduce offshore field utilization, stressing charter renewal economics at contract expiry.

### 4. **LNG — Cheniere Energy Inc.**
*Largest U.S. LNG exporter; operates Sabine Pass and Corpus Christi liquefaction terminals*

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| EV | $85,070mm |
| EV/EBITDA | 14.1× |
| Price | $255.83 |
| EPS | $5.71 |

**Thesis Points**
1. **Volume growth from Corpus Christi Stage 3**: Trains 1–3 of the ~11 MTPA Stage 3 expansion are in commissioning; each incremental train adds ~$400–500mm of high-margin EBITDA as it reaches plateau, with limited incremental capex beyond already-committed spend.
2. **U.S. export dominance structural**: The U.S. at 110.74 Mt is the world's largest LNG exporter; Cheniere captures ~40% of U.S. export capacity. European structural demand for non-Russian LNG is a 10–15 year secular tailwind directly into Cheniere's order book.
3. **SPA contract repricing**: As legacy SPAs signed at Henry Hub + $2–3/mmBtu expire over 2027–2033, Cheniere can reprice into a structurally higher LNG market, materially lifting per-unit margins on re-contracted volumes.
4. **Capital return inflection**: Management committed to returning $20bn+ to shareholders through 2026 via buybacks and dividends; with >60% of free cash flow directed to returns, EPS compounding is significant even without volume growth.
5. **FID pipeline**: Sabine Pass Trains 7–9 (proposed) represent optionality on the next U.S. export wave — Cheniere has the permitted land, infrastructure, and customer relationships to move faster than greenfield competitors.

**Key Risks**
- Henry Hub gas price spike compresses variable margin on SPAs with fixed liquefaction fees — a $1/mmBtu increase in domestic gas costs reduces EBITDA by ~$300–400mm.
- U.S. political/regulatory risk: any reversal of LNG export permitting policy (as briefly occurred in early 2024) clouds the investment case.
- At 14× EV/EBITDA, limited multiple expansion room; the thesis is primarily a cash flow compounding and volume growth story, not a re-rating.

### 5. **DLNG — Dynagas LNG Partners LP**
*High-yield, distressed-value play; 6 ice-class LNG carriers on long-term charters to Gazprom and others*

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| EV | $419mm |
| EV/EBITDA | 4.0× |
| Price | $3.65 |
| EPS | $1.53 |

**Thesis Points**
1. **Extreme valuation discount**: At 4.0× EV/EBITDA and a P/E below 2.5×, DLNG is priced for near-terminal distress despite generating positive earnings — any resolution of the Gazprom charter overhang unlocks significant value.
2. **Ice-class scarcity value**: Arctic-capable LNG tonnage is among the most technically complex and scarce globally — fewer than 20 ice-class LNG carriers exist. As Arctic LNG routes develop (independent of Gazprom), these assets have strategic optionality.
3. **Debt restructuring catalyst**: DLNG completed a credit facility refinancing in 2023; further deleveraging from current EBITDA generation could trigger distribution reinstatement — a binary re-rating event.
4. **Geopolitical resolution option**: Sanctions normalization with Russia (tail scenario, but not zero probability over a 3–5 year horizon) would restore Gazprom charter revenues to full contractual value, eliminating the primary overhang.
5. **Asset liquidation floor**: At 4× EBITDA, even a distressed asset sale of the 6 carriers at market values (~$180–220mm per modern unit) implies significant upside over the current EV.

**Key Risks**
- **Gazprom sanctions risk is existential**: Any escalation of U.S./EU secondary sanctions on DLNG's Russian charters could force charter termination and immediate cash flow collapse.
- LP/MLP structure with suspended distribution has driven away yield investors; the unit may remain in the "penalty box" indefinitely regardless of fundamentals.
- Refinancing wall: if credit markets tighten before the next facility rollover, DLNG's leverage becomes a liquidity crisis, not just an overhang.
- Sponsor (Dynagas Holding) incentive misalignment — management fees continue regardless of unitholder returns.
