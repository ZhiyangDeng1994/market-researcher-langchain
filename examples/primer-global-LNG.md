# global LNG — primer

## Overview
- Global LNG trade grew 2.4% in 2024, reaching 411.24 million tonnes and linking 22 exporting markets with 48 importing markets. The market was valued at USD 133.10 billion in 2024 and is projected to reach USD 199.75 billion by 2030 at a 7.0% CAGR. — *TechSci Research / International Gas Union, 2025*
- The Panama Canal drought (2023–2024) caused LNG transits to fall by up to 73% compared with pre-drought averages. The Canal Authority cut daily transits from 36–38 vessels to as few as 18 per day by February 2024 — a record low. Even after water levels normalized, LNG carriers continued using the longer Cape of Good Hope route and have not returned to pre-drought transit levels. — *CNBC / Panama Canal Authority, September 2025; Peterson Institute for International Economics, 2025*
- In 2024, U.S.-origin LNG bound for Asia via the Panama Canal dropped by 80% compared with prior-year averages, with volumes instead rerouting around Africa's Cape of Good Hope — adding significant voyage distance, time, and cost. — *Atlantic Council, 2025*
- The Strait of Hormuz represents a critical single-point-of-failure: approximately 20% of global LNG trade (around 85.93 MTPA in 2024) must transit the strait, almost entirely from Qatar and the UAE. Qatar alone accounted for nearly 19% of global LNG exports in 2025. South Asia is most exposed — Qatar and the UAE supply roughly 53% of India's LNG imports, 72% of Bangladesh's, and 99% of Pakistan's. — *Kpler, March 2026; IEEFA, June 2025; Springer / IGU, 2025*
- The global LNG fleet comprised over 830 vessels as of early 2025, but rapid growth in global LNG trade could lead to a tighter shipping market post-2028. Only 234 newbuild LNG carriers are scheduled for delivery between 2026 and 2030, which analysts say is insufficient to handle the 229 million tonnes per year of new LNG export capacity already sanctioned globally. 'There definitely looks like there is going to be a shortage,' said the CEO of Capital Clean Energy Carriers at the World LNG Summit. — *IEA Gas 2025 report; bne IntelliNews / Argus Media, December 2025*
- A wave of vessel scrapping is compounding the shipping gap: 14 steam turbine LNG carriers were sold for demolition in 2025 alone — nearly triple the annual average from 2020 to 2024 — as ageing vessels are deemed obsolete due to high boil-off rates and limited trading flexibility. — *bne IntelliNews / Argus Media, December 2025*
- Feedgas supply issues at legacy LNG producers — particularly in Africa and Southeast Asia — are a structural bottleneck projected to reduce global LNG production by nearly 20 bcm per year by 2030. In Australia, declining upstream deliverability at projects including North West Shelf, Darwin LNG, Gorgon, and Prelude FLNG is dampening export growth potential through to 2030. — *IEA Gas 2025 report; IEA Gas Market Report Q1-2026*
- Norway's Hammerfest LNG suffered a delayed restart that lowered output by 44% year-on-year in Q1–Q3 2025. Algeria's LNG exports have trended below prior-year levels since Q2 2024 and were down 22% year-on-year. Australia's exports declined 3% year-on-year due to ongoing decline at North West Shelf and a month-long maintenance shut-in at Ichthys LNG. — *IEA Gas 2025 report (IEA, 2025)*
- LNG infrastructure project costs rose sharply, with material costs increasing 12–20% between 2022 and 2024. Geopolitical disruptions impacted 15% of LNG trade routes in 2023, causing rerouting of over 80 LNG cargoes. Around 25% of importing regions still depend entirely on third-party infrastructure for LNG storage. — *Market Reports World / LNG Market Research Report, 2024*
- In 2024, no new U.S. LNG projects reached a final investment decision (FID) — the lowest number of FID announcements since 2020 (only 15 MTPA globally reached FID, vs. nearly 60 MTPA in 2023) — after regulatory uncertainty froze investment. This pause created a gap in the new-supply pipeline before the policy environment shifted in 2025. — *Gaffney Cline / Alaska LNG Project Summary Report, December 2025; GECF Gas Exporting Countries Forum, December 2025*
- Subsea equipment and gas-related infrastructure remain severely constrained in the supply chain even as upstream bottlenecks ease. Rystad Energy analysts described 2025 as a 'very disappointing' year for project activity, and note that procurement tightness for LNG and petrochemical infrastructure is not expected to ease broadly until 2026. — *Energy Chamber of Trinidad and Tobago / Rystad Energy, October 2025*
- U.S. Gulf Coast LNG export terminals are currently operating at or near full capacity, meaning significant export growth is impossible without additional infrastructure. Gulf Coast port capacity, pipeline bottlenecks, and insufficient terminal capacity are identified as the primary constraints on U.S. LNG supply growth; Permian Basin gas prices fell below zero for 42% of trading days in 2024 due to takeaway capacity constraints. — *American Action Forum, 2025; U.S. Department of Energy / NPC Permitting Report, December 2025*
- Europe's LNG regasification capacity growth is decelerating sharply — rising 13% in 2023, 8% in 2024, and just ~2% in 2025 — as future gas demand outlooks weaken. Several planned terminals have been cancelled or shelved (e.g., Germany's Mukran FSRU, France's Le Havre FSRU), raising stranded-asset risk and reducing flexibility during potential supply shocks. — *IEEFA European LNG Tracker, 2025; European Gas Hub, 2025*
- Global LNG liquefaction capacity is forecast to expand by around 300 bcm per year by 2030 versus 2024 levels, led by the U.S. and Qatar (accounting for over 70% of additions). However, net LNG supply growth will be only ~250 bcm after accounting for legacy producer declines, ramp-up rates, and utilization factors — meaning bottlenecks at legacy facilities materially erode the headline capacity expansion. — *IEA Gas 2025 report (IEA, 2025)*

# Global LNG Supply Chain Bottlenecks — Investment Shortlist

---

## 1. **Cheniere Energy (LNG)** — *Long*

**EV/EBITDA: 14.1x | EPS: $5.71**

### Thesis Points
1. **Capacity utilization moat**: U.S. Gulf Coast terminals running at full capacity with locked-in long-term SPAs; volume risk is near-zero through ~2030 across Sabine Pass + Corpus Christi
2. **New supply pipeline**: Train 3 at Corpus Christi mid-scale is the most shovel-ready incremental U.S. LNG addition; policy tailwinds post-2025 regulatory thaw accelerate FID cadence
3. **Pricing structure insulation**: ~85% of volumes contracted on fixed-fee tolling; company captures margin regardless of spot LNG price volatility created by the bottlenecks described
4. **Route disruption beneficiary**: Panama Canal rerouting extends voyage times, tightening effective shipping capacity and increasing per-cargo economics for U.S. exporters with flexible destination clauses
5. **Balance sheet trajectory**: ~$20B in cumulative distributable cash flow guided through 2027; buyback + dividend growth story as leverage declines

### Key Risks
- Feedgas pipeline takeaway constraints (Permian negative pricing 42% of days in 2024) could cap upstream supply economics  `[UNSOURCED]`
- Regulatory re-freeze (permitting/FERC) on expansion trains
- Sustained LNG spot price collapse reduces re-contracting leverage post-2030

---

## 2. **Golar LNG (GLNG)** — *Long*

**EV/EBITDA: 26.8x | EPS: $1.26**

### Thesis Points
1. **FLNG scarcity premium**: Hilli Episeyo (Cameroon) and Gimi (Greater Tortue) represent the only commercially operating FLNG units in the Atlantic Basin; replication lead time is 5–7 years
2. **Bottleneck arbitrageur**: FLNG technology bypasses the exact infrastructure bottlenecks (subsea tightness, terminal construction cost inflation, permitting delays) constraining conventional greenfield LNG
3. **Gimi ramp-up optionality**: Gimi reached first LNG in early 2024 under a 20-year fixed-fee contract with BP/Kosmos; earnings step-up as utilization scales provides multi-year EPS catalyst
4. **Fleet conversion pipeline**: Management has publicly guided toward a third FLNG conversion; in a market where Rystad flags 2025–2026 as severely constrained for LNG infrastructure procurement, Golar's in-house conversion expertise is genuinely scarce
5. **Shipping spin-off clean story**: Post-Cool Company demerger, GLNG is a pure-play FLNG operator with a cleaner capital allocation narrative

### Key Risks
- Single-asset concentration (Hilli represents majority of current EBITDA); any prolonged outage is highly material
- West Africa political/operational risk (Cameroon, Mauritania/Senegal)
- Elevated EV/EBITDA multiple leaves little room for execution slippage on Gimi ramp

---

## 3. **Flex LNG (FLNG)** — *Long*

**EV/EBITDA: 13.7x | EPS: $1.40**

### Thesis Points
1. **Fleet quality premium**: 13 modern tri-fuel diesel electric (TFDE) and two-stroke vessels — zero steam turbine exposure; directly benefits from the scrapping wave eliminating 14+ older vessels in 2025
2. **Post-2028 shipping squeeze**: With only 234 newbuilds scheduled vs. 229 MTPA of new export capacity already sanctioned, modern tonnage holders gain pricing power; Flex's fleet age profile positions it to re-contract at materially higher rates
3. **Cape of Good Hope rerouting adds ton-miles**: Permanent rerouting away from Panama Canal structurally increases effective fleet demand without adding a single vessel — directly expands revenue per ship per year
4. **Long-term charter coverage**: ~70% of fleet days covered through 2026+ under time charters; visible cash flow supports the $1.60/share annual dividend
5. **Counter-cyclical scrapping beneficiary**: As legacy steam vessels exit, Flex faces fewer competitive alternatives at renewal — charter rate floor rises

### Key Risks
- Spot rate exposure on uncovered fleet days; LNG shipping rates can be highly volatile (fell sharply in H2 2023)
- Newbuild ordering by larger players (MISC, MISC, MOL, NYK) could overshoot if demand disappoints
- South Korean yard delivery delays could temporarily flood the market if multiple vessels deliver simultaneously

---

## 4. **TotalEnergies (TTE)** — *Long*

**EV/EBITDA: 5.7x | EPS: $6.69**

### Thesis Points
1. **Integrated LNG portfolio depth**: ~50 MTPA of equity LNG across Qatar (NFS expansion partner), U.S. (Cameron LNG), Papua New Guinea, and East Africa — the broadest diversification by geography in the listed universe
2. **Qatar NFS expansion direct exposure**: TTE is a partner in Qatar's North Field South expansion, adding ~16 MTPA by 2026–2028; Qatar is the single most important swing supplier through the Hormuz corridor
3. **Valuation discount to peers**: At 5.7x EV/EBITDA vs. Cheniere at 14x and SHEL at ~6x, TTE's LNG optionality is embedded nearly for free relative to its integrated peer group
4. **Feedgas diversification hedge**: Unlike Southeast Asian and Australian legacy producers facing structural decline, TTE's Qatar and U.S. positions are in growth or stable basins
5. **European regasification rationalization is not a TTE risk**: TTE's exposure is on the supply/export side; stranded regasification risk sits with importers, not producers

### Key Risks
- Algeria exposure: TTE's Sonatrach relationship ties it to a producer whose exports are down 22% YoY — some upstream concentration risk  `[UNSOURCED]`
- Hormuz black swan: Qatar concentration means a strait closure would impair TTE's largest single LNG equity tranche
- ESG/capital allocation pressure from European institutional investors could constrain LNG-weighted capital deployment

## 5. **NextDecade (NEXT)** — *Speculative Long / Event-Driven*

**EV: $13.7B (negative EBITDA) | Price: $7.61**

### Thesis Points
1. **Rio Grande LNG Train 1–3 FID achieved**: With Train 1 under construction and Trains 2–3 FID'd, NEXT transitions from developer to constructor — the highest de-risking inflection point in the LNG project lifecycle
2. **Direct beneficiary of 2025 policy shift**: Post-regulatory thaw, NEXT is among the first movers with permits in hand; competing projects facing permitting queues cannot replicate this position for 2–3 years
3. **Carbon capture differentiation (REACT)**: Rio Grande's embedded CO₂ capture technology is a genuine commercial differentiator for European buyers under CBAM and corporate net-zero mandates — enables premium pricing vs. conventional LNG
4. **Permian Basin feedgas access**: Located in the Rio Grande Valley with direct pipeline access to the Permian; while takeaway constraints are real, NEXT's position benefits from the same gas price depression (sub-zero 42% of days) as a buyer
5. **Equity re-rating from construction progress**: As concrete milestones are hit (first steel, first LNG), the stock has historically re-rated sharply; still trading well below pre-permit-pause highs

### Key Risks
- **Negative EBITDA and significant execution risk**: Any construction cost overrun (infrastructure costs +12–20% since 2022) hits equity holders hard given leverage in the capital structure
- Offtake counterparty concentration — a credit event at a key SPA holder could unwind project financing
- Prolonged low spot LNG prices reduce re-contracting appetite for uncommitted tranches
- Supply chain tightness (Rystad: "very disappointing" 2025 for procurement) could push first LNG dates right
