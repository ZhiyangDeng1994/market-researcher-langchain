# global shipping and logistics — primer

## Overview
- The Shanghai Containerized Freight Index (SCFI) averaged 2,496 points in 2024 — up approximately 149% from the 2023 average — peaking at 3,600 points in mid-2024, its highest level since the COVID-19 logistics crunch of 2021–2022. — *UNCTAD, Review of Maritime Transport 2025 (Chapter III: Freight Rates and Maritime Transport Costs), September 2025*
- Container shipping prices in 2024 were more than double the levels recorded in 2023, peaking in July 2024 at just 25% below the historic 2021 supply-bottleneck highs — driven primarily by Red Sea/Suez Canal disruptions forcing rerouting via the Cape of Good Hope. — *OECD, 'The Impact of Container Shipping Costs on Import and Consumer Prices', 2025*
- Rerouting ships around the Cape of Good Hope instead of the Suez Canal added 4,000–6,000 extra nautical miles and 10–15 additional transit days per voyage, while increasing journey times from Shanghai to North European and Mediterranean ports by 30–45%. — *OECD, 'The Impact of Container Shipping Costs on Import and Consumer Prices', 2025*
- By Q4 2025, the Drewry World Container Index had fallen to approximately $1,669 per 40-foot container — a 20-month low — with the Shanghai–Los Angeles route down 58% year-on-year to $2,196 per container, near or below the ~$2,200 break-even threshold for large carriers. — *Exim News Service / Drewry World Container Index, cited in Lars Jensen (LinkedIn), October 2025*
- Structural overcapacity is the primary downward driver: global container fleet capacity is projected to expand 6.7% in 2025 and 4.0% in 2026 (Clarksons Research), while demand growth lags at roughly 2.6–3.0%, creating a persistent supply-demand imbalance. — *UNCTAD, Review of Maritime Transport 2025, September 2025*
- The container shipping order book-to-fleet ratio stood at 31.6% in 2025 (up from 27.5% at the start of 2023), with approximately 2.1 million TEU delivered in 2025, declining to 1.7 million TEU in 2026 before surging to 2.8 million TEU in 2027 and 3.5 million TEU in 2028. — *Clarksons Securities, cited in Maritime Gateway, 'Container Shipping Forecast 2026', 2025*
- BIMCO estimates that a return to normal Red Sea/Suez Canal routing — if it were to occur — would lower effective ship demand by approximately 10%, releasing more than 2 million TEU of absorbed capacity back into an already oversupplied market and exerting further downward pressure on rates. — *BIMCO Container Shipping Market Overview & Outlook, June 2025 (Container News)*
- Global container trade reached 126.75 million TEU in January–August 2025, marking 4.4% year-over-year growth — decisively outperforming pessimistic early-year forecasts that had predicted flat or negative growth — despite unprecedented US tariff implementations and geopolitical tensions. — *VIZION, 'Global Container Trade: 2025 Performance Review and 2026 Forecasts', November 25, 2025*
- US full-year 2025 container import volumes are forecast at 24.1 million TEU, down 5.6% from 25.5 million TEU in 2024, driven by tariff-related inventory front-loading in Q1–Q2 (+15–20% above normal) followed by a sharp Q3 correction of -10 to -15%. — *VIZION, 'Global Container Trade: 2025 Performance Review and 2026 Forecasts', November 25, 2025*
- Major carriers reported sharply compressed margins in 2025: CMA CGM's full-year 2025 maritime EBITDA fell to $7.9 billion from $11.2 billion in 2024 (a 29% decline), with average revenue per TEU down 8.7% to $1,414; COSCO's Q3 2025 net profit fell 55% year-on-year. — *CMA CGM Group Full-Year 2025 Financial Results (Container News); Tradlinx Q3 2025 Carrier Scorecard*
- The EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS) coverage for maritime shipping rose from 40% of verified emissions in 2024 to 70% in 2025, with 100% coverage beginning in 2026; combined with the new FuelEU Maritime regulation, EU regulations alone are estimated to add $6.1 billion to industry costs in 2025. — *NAPA, 'EU ETS Shipping Compliance: How to Prepare for the First EUA Deadline in 2025', September 4, 2025*
- The FuelEU Maritime regulation, effective January 2025, requires ships over 5,000 gross tonnage calling at EU ports to reduce GHG intensity by 2% from 2025, scaling to 6% by 2030, 14.5% by 2035, and 80% by 2050, covering CO₂, methane, and nitrous oxide on a well-to-wake basis. — *Breakthrough Fuel / Port-Xchange, 'EU 2025 Maritime Regulations: Decarbonization & Industry', 2025*
- The global shipping and logistics market was valued at $280.4 billion in 2024 and is projected to reach $362.8 billion by 2031, growing at a CAGR of 3.8%; the container shipping sub-segment alone is forecast to grow from $119.7 billion in 2025 to $142.1 billion by 2031 at a 2.92% CAGR. — *Valuates Reports, 'Global Shipping and Logistics Market Research Report 2025', September 12, 2025; Mordor Intelligence, 'Container Shipping Market Size', 2025*
- Maritime trade growth is expected to slow sharply to just 0.5% in 2025, down from 2.2% in 2024, as geopolitical tensions, rising costs, tariff uncertainty, and supply chain fragility weigh on volumes — with UNCTAD describing freight rate volatility as 'the new normal' across all shipping segments. — *UNCTAD, 'Maritime Trade Under Pressure – Growth Set to Stall in 2025', Review of Maritime Transport 2025, September 24, 2025*

## Ideas
# Shortlist: Global Shipping & Logistics — Container Rate Normalization

---

## Macro Framework

The sector sits at a **rate-normalization inflection**: 2024's Red Sea–driven SCFI spike (~2,496 avg, peak 3,600) is unwinding hard into structural oversupply. Fleet capacity grows 6.7% in 2025 vs. ~2.6–3.0% demand growth. Drewry WCI already at $1,669 — near/below break-even for large carriers. The **2027–2028 orderbook surge (2.8–3.5M TEU/year)** makes a durable rate recovery unlikely before late-decade. This creates a bifurcated opportunity: **shorts/puts on spot-exposed carriers** vs. **longs on contracted/asset-light models** insulated from rate volatility.

---

## 1. ZIM Integrated Shipping Services (ZIM) — **Bear / Short**

*EV $6.8B | EV/EBITDA ~14.5x | EPS $0.83*

**Thesis Points:**
1. **Pure spot-rate exposure**: ZIM deliberately runs ~55–65% of its book on short-term/spot contracts — the highest among major carriers — making it the most leveraged name to SCFI downside as rates normalize toward $1,500–1,800.
2. **Asset-light via charters = fixed cost trap**: ZIM owns very few vessels; it charters capacity at rates locked during the 2022–2024 peak. As spot revenue collapses faster than charter costs roll off, margin compression is asymmetric and severe.
3. **Dividend policy is a mirage at trough rates**: ZIM's variable dividend (tied to net income) was a bull-market story. With COSCO net profit -55% YoY and CMA CGM EBITDA -29%, ZIM's thin $0.83 EPS leaves little dividend cushion and may eliminate the yield thesis entirely.
4. **EV/EBITDA of 14.5x is expensive for a cyclical trough approach**: DAC and GSL trade at 3.5x and 3.4x respectively — ZIM's premium is unjustifiable if EBITDA halves again in 2026 as the orderbook surge hits.
5. **US tariff volume shock**: US imports -5.6% in 2025 with a sharp Q3 correction (-10 to -15%). ZIM has meaningful transpacific exposure; Shanghai–LA route already -58% YoY, near/below the $2,200 break-even.

**Key Risks to Short:**
- Red Sea normalization *delayed indefinitely* → Cape routing absorbs ~2M+ TEU, keeping effective supply tighter than orderbook implies
- Surprise demand spike from tariff front-loading round 2 (e.g., new US–China trade deal window)
- Short squeeze on high short interest / volatile dividend announcement

## 2. Danaos Corporation (DAC) — **Bull / Long**

*EV $2.4B | EV/EBITDA ~3.5x | EPS $28.33*

**Thesis Points:**
1. **Fixed-rate charter backlog insulates from spot collapse**: Danaos operates as a containership lessor, not a carrier. Its vessels are locked into multi-year time charters with liner companies (Evergreen, MSC, ZIM), generating contracted revenue streams largely immune to near-term SCFI moves.
2. **3.5x EV/EBITDA is deeply discounted vs. logistics peers (CHRW 27.9x, EXPD 19.8x)**: Even applying a 40% cyclical discount to logistics comps, DAC screens as 2–3 turns cheap — the market is pricing in charter renewal risk that is 2–3 years away for most of the fleet.
3. **Balance sheet deleveraging creates capital return optionality**: DAC has aggressively paid down debt and returned capital via buybacks. Net debt/EBITDA is among the lowest in the containership lessor space, providing downside protection and flexibility to acquire vessels cheaply as distressed carriers sell.
4. **Newbuild discipline**: Unlike carriers, DAC has not over-ordered. Its orderbook exposure is modest relative to fleet size, meaning no self-inflicted dilution of charter rates when the 2027–2028 delivery wave hits competitors.
5. **EU ETS cost pass-through**: As a vessel owner leasing to operators, many of DAC's charter structures shift fuel/emissions compliance costs to charterers, limiting direct EU ETS / FuelEU Maritime liability estimated at $6.1B industry-wide.

**Key Risks:**
- Charter renewal risk: as contracts expire 2026–2028, renewal rates will reset materially lower in an oversupplied market
- Counterparty risk: ZIM (a key charterer) faces its own financial stress; charter defaults are not zero-probability
- Illiquidity premium / thin float keeps the stock chronically "cheap" without a catalyst

## 3. Global Ship Lease (GSL) — **Bull / Long**

*EV $1.6B | EV/EBITDA ~3.4x | EPS $10.43*

**Thesis Points:**
1. **Contracted revenue visibility at trough multiples**: GSL's charter backlog provides 2–3 years of locked cash flows, with weighted average remaining charter duration of ~2.5 years. At 3.4x EBITDA, the market is pricing near-zero terminal value — unreasonable even in a soft rate environment.
2. **Mid-size vessel focus is a structural advantage**: GSL concentrates on 2,200–11,000 TEU vessels — the "workhorse" segment used for intra-regional trade and feeder routes. These are less exposed to the mega-ship oversupply problem concentrated in the 15,000–24,000 TEU ultra-large carrier (ULC) segment flooding the transpacific.
3. **Aggressive buyback program**: GSL has consistently repurchased shares, shrinking the float. At current prices, buybacks are highly accretive and signal management's view that intrinsic value is materially above market price.
4. **Dividend yield provides floor**: GSL offers a meaningful dividend yield (historically 7–10%+) that partially compensates investors for waiting through the rate trough — unlike carrier peers whose dividends are variable and collapsing.
5. **Potential consolidation target**: At $1.6B EV, GSL is bite-sized for larger shipping conglomerates (CMRE parent Costamare, or private equity) seeking to acquire contracted cash flows at distressed multiples.

**Key Risks:**
- Same charter renewal/repricing risk as DAC — amplified by slightly shorter remaining durations
- Leverage is higher than DAC; a prolonged rate depression could stress refinancing
- Small/mid cap illiquidity; institutional flows out of shipping can disproportionately pressure the stock

## 4. Expeditors International (EXPD) — **Bull / Long**

*EV $22.5B | EV/EBITDA ~19.8x | EPS $6.17*

**Thesis Points:**
1. **Asset-light freight forwarding = natural beneficiary of rate normalization**: EXPD earns a spread between carrier rates and shipper-paid rates. In falling-rate environments, buy-low/sell-still-contracted dynamics temporarily expand gross yields; longer term, volumes grow as lower shipping costs stimulate trade.
2. **Counter-cyclical volume upside**: The OECD documents that container cost pass-through to consumer prices takes 12–18 months. As 2024's rate spike flows through into 2025–2026 import softness, a normalization back toward $1,500–1,800/TEU should stimulate 3–5% incremental volume demand — directly benefiting forwarder throughput.
3. **Pristine balance sheet with net cash**: EXPD is one of the few logistics companies with essentially no net debt, giving it acquisition firepower and the ability to return capital (buybacks + dividends) throughout the cycle without financial stress.
4. **Disciplined cost structure**: EXPD's employee-ownership model and famously lean cost culture mean operating leverage works in both directions — margins held better than peers during the 2023 rate collapse and should recover faster in a normalized environment.
5. **Regulatory complexity creates forwarder value**: EU ETS, FuelEU Maritime, and tariff rerouting create enormous compliance and customs complexity for shippers. Sophisticated forwarders with compliance capabilities (EXPD's core competency) command pricing power above simple capacity brokers.

**Key Risks:**
- At 19.8x EV/EBITDA, EXPD is priced for quality — limited upside if normalization is slower than expected  `[UNSOURCED]`
- US import volume decline (-5.6% in 2025) directly compresses transpacific forwarding revenues in the near term  `[UNSOURCED]`
- Competition from digital freight platforms (Flexport, Freightos) eroding margin on commoditized lanes

## 5. C.H. Robinson (CHRW) — **Bear / Avoid / Mild Short**

*EV $24.8B | EV/EBITDA ~27.9x | EPS $4.88*

**Thesis Points:**
1. **Premium multiple is difficult to justify in a normalizing freight cycle**: CHRW trades at 27.9x EV/EBITDA — the highest in the comp set — despite being a volume-driven trucking/ocean brokerage business whose revenue per shipment tracks freight rates directionally. Rate normalization compresses both the top line and the brokerage spread.
2. **Truckload brokerage headwinds compound ocean softness**: CHRW's core North American surface business faces its own overcapacity problem (US trucking market has been in a prolonged freight recession since 2022). Ocean normalization removes the one segment that was buoying results.
3. **Digital disruption is a genuine structural threat**: Unlike EXPD's deep customs/compliance moat, CHRW's value proposition in standard brokerage is increasingly replicable by algorithmic platforms. Margin erosion risk is not fully priced at 27.9x.
4. **EPS of $4.88 implies 40.5x P/E — a growth stock multiple for a cyclical broker**: If EBITDA reverts to mid-cycle norms (~$650–700M vs. $887M current), the stock de-rates sharply even without multiple compression.
5. **US tariff volume shock hits the core customer**: CHRW's customer base skews toward US importers. The -5.6% import volume forecast for 2025 with a -10 to -15% Q3 correction is a direct headwind to shipment count, the primary revenue driver.

**Key Risks to Bear:**
- CHRW has a history of operational self-help; new management has been cutting costs — margin recovery could surprise
- A US–China trade deal or tariff rollback could spike import volumes sharply, benefiting CHRW disproportionately
- 27.9x may reflect a sum-of-parts (tech platform + brokerage) that the market is beginning to price  `[UNSOURCED]`
